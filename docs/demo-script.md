# BCBS 239 demo script — v0.1

For a Deployment Strategist. Roughly 25 minutes, seven scenes, three pillars.

**The arc:** show a real gap → build the governance that closes it → show what
is still genuinely missing. It opens on tension and ends on a remediation list,
not on "all green."

**The claim:** *upload a regulation, and build your governance framework in
conversation with it.*

---

## Before the room

| Prerequisite | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Data source connected, metadata harvested | UI | MCF Snowflake, `alation://data/2` |
| Custom fields created | UI | `PII Classification`, `Sensitivity Classification`, `CDM (contains CDE)` |
| **Curation Automation run, with vertical-specific prompts** | UI | See the note below — this is a best practice, not a detail |
| Policy group exists | UI | Groups have no create API. `BCBS 239`, id 1 |
| Agents + tools deployed | kit | `./run.sh deploy …`, `./run.sh tool deploy …` |
| **One published** CDE overlay standard | CDM, once | Permanent. Published in setup, reused every run |
| 3 CDEs, drafts, PDEs discovered | kit | `cde apply` + `cde map` |

Everything above is **setup**, and Aaron's ruling covers it: setup may be
scripts, the end-to-end experience must be in the UI.

**Reset between demos:** delete the Draft standard from scene 4 and the policies
from scene 3. Both are deletable. The published standard stays and is reused —
that is what makes this repeatable rather than one-and-done.

---

## 1 — The catalog (2 min) · *context*

Open the data source. Tables, columns, lineage — technical metadata, harvested.

> "This is what most catalogs are. It tells you what exists. It doesn't tell you
> what matters, who owns it, or whether a regulator would accept it."

Sets up why the rest is needed. Do not linger.

---

## 2 — Curation (3 min) · *pillar 1: openness*

Show the curation rule and what it produced — descriptions, owners, trust flags
and classification fields that a steward didn't hand-write.

> "A catalog is only as good as its enriched content. This is how you get there
> without a year of stewardship."

**The best-practice point, and say it explicitly:** run **vertical-specific**
Curation Automation prompts against the customer's own data, not the stock ones.
Ours came from `#industry-vertical-financialservices`. A financial-services
prompt set describes columns in the vocabulary the *regulation* uses — which is
what every downstream step reads. Generic prompts produce generic descriptions
and the compliance layer inherits that vagueness.

Also worth naming: the three custom fields — `PII Classification`,
`Sensitivity Classification`, `CDM (contains CDE)`. Curation populates them, and
`CDM (contains CDE)` is the flag that connects this scene to scene 6.

> ⚠️ **Causal claim pending measurement.** Whether curation improves CDE
> discovery is being tested — before/after on the same three CDEs with keywords
> held constant (baseline 7 / 9 / 4, recorded in CLAUDE.md). Until that lands,
> say "richer metadata gives the agents more to work with" rather than claiming
> it drives discovery.

---

## 3 — The gap, before anything exists (4 min) · *the hook*

Agent Studio chat. Run the gap analysis against the catalog as it stands.

> "Here's BCBS 239. Here's your catalog. What can you actually evidence?"

The output names what's missing and why it matters — not "6 gaps found" but
*"you cannot evidence your Principle 4 completeness claim, because there is no
business line, legal entity or geography attribute on any risk record."*

**This is the strongest moment in the demo.** Let it sit.

> ⚠️ **Re-measure before use.** The "5 of 11" figures predate the SQL pack.
> `CPTY_ID`, `EXPOSURE_AMT` and `GL_RECON_KEY` are now present. Whatever number
> you say has to be true on the instance in front of you.

---

## 4 — Policies, in conversation (5 min) · *pillar 1: openness*

Same chat. Paste the BCBS 239 text.

The agent proposes the policies the regulation implies — one per principle,
each cited to numbered paragraphs. You review the list on screen. You say yes.
It creates them: shell, title, description, policy group, one at a time.

> "You didn't fill in a form. You agreed to a list."

Open Policy Center. Seven policies, each quoting the paragraphs it derives from.

**What to point at:** the verbatim quotations. Every one is reproduced from the
source document by code, not written by a model — so the citation cannot drift.

> 📌 Today the interpretation runs in the kit and only creation is
> conversational. Folding both into one chat agent is the next build, and it is
> what makes this scene land as written.

---

## 5 — Standards, generated by Alation (4 min) · *pillar 2: governed*

CDM → Standards → **Add new Standard** → pick a policy → **Create**.

Alation's own AI reads the policy body and derives the requirements. Wait for it.

> "We didn't write these. Alation read the policy and worked out what a steward
> has to be able to prove."

Open it: requirements, attestation fields, pickers with real answer sets.
**Trim it live** — drop the requirements that are enterprise-level rather than
per-element, and say why. Then explain that publishing is the step that makes it
govern, and that it's deliberate and reviewed.

**Delete this draft after the demo.** That is the reset.

---

## 6 — CDE Manager (5 min) · *pillar 2: governed*

Open a CDE — Counterparty Identifier.

- **Standards** — Baseline Metadata, Risk Assessment Framework, and the BCBS 239
  overlay, with the attestation questions its steward must answer
- **Physical Data Elements** — the columns, found by CDM's own discovery from the
  element's synonyms, marked `Suggested` for a human to accept
- **Data Quality** — the monitors and their scores

> "This is the governed end state. The regulation is on the left, the column is
> on the right, and every step between them is visible."

Show the workflow stepper — Candidate → Draft → In Review → Certified — and that
everything here arrived as a **draft** awaiting a human.

> ⚠️ **DQ monitors are not built.** The Data Quality tile reads `N/A`. Either
> build them before demoing this scene, or say plainly that monitors are the
> next step and don't open the tile.

---

## 7 — The loop (2 min) · *pillar 3: feedback*

Re-run the gap analysis.

What you built is now covered. What remains is a **data** gap, not a governance
gap — and that distinction is the payoff.

> "That's your remediation list. And it's not a one-off — run it monthly, or
> when the regulation changes, and it tells you what moved."

End there. Do not end on "no gaps."

---

## Close

Three claims, in the customer's terms:

- **Open** — your regulation, your policy pack, read by the platform. Not ours.
- **Governed** — traceable from a paragraph in a regulation to a column in a
  warehouse, with a named human at every gate.
- **Feedback loop** — it re-runs, and it tells you what changed.

---

## Known seams

Honest list, because an SE walking into this should know where it's thin.

1. **Scene 4 is split** across kit and chat today.
2. **Scene 6 has no DQ scores.** Biggest hole, and it's in the pillar Aaron
   cares most about.
3. **Scene 2's causal claim is unmeasured.**
4. **Scene 3's numbers are stale.**
5. **Real PDF ingestion is blocked** on an FDE feature flag, so the regulation
   arrives as pasted text rather than as an S3 document in the catalog. That's
   the version of scene 4 you actually want.
6. **Publishing a standard is permanent.** State it to the customer before
   running anything in their instance.

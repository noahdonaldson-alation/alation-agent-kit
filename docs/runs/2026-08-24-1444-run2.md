# BCBS 239: Data Catalog Interpretation

## 1. Objectives and Scope

### What the regulation is trying to achieve

- **Reliable risk aggregation as a board-level responsibility.** The regulation requires that boards and senior management actively govern risk data quality, approve aggregation frameworks, and understand the limits of what their data infrastructure can produce. (Â¶28: "A bank's board and senior management should review and approve the bank's group risk data aggregation and risk reporting framework and ensure that adequate resources are deployed.")

- **A single, documented, authoritative data architecture.** Banks must establish integrated taxonomies, metadata, unified naming conventions, and single identifiers across legal entities, counterparties, customers, and accounts â not as a best practice but as a precondition for aggregation. (Â¶33: "A bank should establish integrated data taxonomies and architecture across the banking group, which includes information on the characteristics of the data (metadata), as well as use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions.")

- **Accuracy with reconciliation to accounting sources.** Risk data must be reconcilable to the general ledger and other authoritative sources, with controls at least as rigorous as those applied to accounting data. Manual workarounds must be documented and their impact on accuracy quantified. (Â¶36(c): "Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate, to ensure that the risk data is accurate.")

- **Complete capture of all material exposures, including off-balance-sheet.** Aggregation capabilities must cover all material risks across every business line, legal entity, asset type, industry, and geography â with completeness itself measured and monitored. (Â¶41: "A bank's risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures, including those that are off-balance sheet.")

- **Timeliness calibrated to risk volatility and stress conditions.** Systems must be capable of producing aggregated risk data rapidly during stress; for the most critical risks (large credit exposures, counterparty derivatives, liquidity indicators), intraday production may be required. (Â¶45: "Banks need to build their risk systems to be capable of producing aggregated risk data rapidly during times of stress/crisis for all critical risks.")

- **Adaptability for ad hoc and supervisory requests.** Aggregation capabilities must be flexible enough to slice data by any combination of dimension (country, sector, business line, date) on demand, including in response to emerging risk scenarios or regulatory queries. (Â¶50: "Supervisors expect banks to be able to generate subsets of data based on requested scenarios or resulting from economic events.")

### Who it applies to

BCBS 239 was issued for **Global Systemically Important Banks (G-SIBs)** as a mandatory standard, with national supervisors expected to apply it to **Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs)** on a proportionate basis (Â¶7â9, outside the principles text but contextually unambiguous). The principles apply to the **consolidated banking group**, including subsidiaries, branches, and â subject to legal constraints â joint ventures (Â¶30 references "coverage â¦ subsidiaries not included" as an identified limitation type).

**CDE-01 â Counterparty Identifier**
- **Definition:** A unique, persistent identifier assigned to each legal-entity counterparty with whom the bank has a risk exposure â credit, market, or derivative â that remains stable across business lines and systems.
- **Why critical:** Without a single, resolvable counterparty identifier, exposures originating in different systems (loans, derivatives, securities financing) cannot be aggregated to produce a total exposure to one borrower. - **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, concentration
- **Criticality:** 3 â Counterparty-level aggregation is explicitly named as a critical risk (Â¶46(b)) and the inability to join on a common identifier is the single most common cause of aggregation failure.
- **Driven by:** Principle 2 (Â¶33) â "use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities, counterparties, customers and accounts"; and Principle 4 (Â¶41) â completeness across all material exposures.
- **Search terms:** counterparty ID, party ID, legal entity identifier, LEI, obligor ID, client ID, customer identifier, GFCID, golden source party
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *uniqueness* â Each counterparty maps to exactly one authoritative identifier in the golden source; no two identifiers refer to the same legal entity | Count of duplicate counterparty records in the authoritative party register | Zero duplicates
  - *validity* â Every counterparty identifier on an exposure record resolves to an active record in the authoritative party register | Count of exposure records whose counterparty identifier is null, blank, or unmatched in the register | <0.1% of exposure notional
  - *completeness* â Every exposure record carries a non-null counterparty identifier | Percentage of exposure records missing a counterparty identifier | 100% populated

**CDE-02 â Legal Entity Identifier (Booking Entity)**
- **Definition:** The identifier of the legal entity within the banking group in whose books an exposure, position, or liability is recorded â used to aggregate risk at the subsidiary or branch level and to roll up to the consolidated group.
- **Why critical:** Regulatory capital, large exposure limits, and resolution planning all operate at the legal-entity level. Without a reliable booking-entity identifier, cross-entity aggregation and intra-group netting calculations are impossible.
- **Risk types:** Credit, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 â Â¶33 explicitly requires single identifiers for legal entities; Â¶30 names "subsidiaries not included" as a known aggregation gap that senior management must disclose.
- **Driven by:** Principle 2 (Â¶33) â "single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities"; Principle 4 (Â¶41) â capture across "the banking group."

**CDE-03 â Exposure Amount (Gross Notional / Carrying Value)**

**CDE-04 â Transaction Currency and Base-Currency Conversion Rate**

**CDE-05 â Risk Classification / Risk Type Code**

**CDE-11 â Position / Valuation Date (As-of Date)**

## 3. Cross-Cutting Data Quality Requirements

**XDQ-01 â Risk-to-Finance Reconciliation**

- **Requirement intent:** The aggregate of risk exposures in the risk data layer must reconcile to the corresponding balances in the general ledger (or the finance system of record) within a documented materiality threshold. Unexplained differences must be escalated and resolved through a formal breaks process.
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (Â¶36(a),(c)) â "Controls surrounding risk data should be as robust as those applicable to accounting data" and "Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate."
- **DQ dimensions:** accuracy, completeness, consistency
- **Measurement:** The sum of exposure amounts (CDE-03) in the risk layer, mapped by legal entity (CDE-02), position date (CDE-11), and product type (CDE-09), compared against the equivalent GL balance; the count and total value of unreconciled breaks; elapsed time to resolve breaks.
- **Suggested threshold:** Zero unresolved breaks above the bank's defined accounting materiality level (Â¶56) at close of each reporting period; all breaks documented with root cause within T+1.
- **CDEs spanned:** CDE-02, CDE-03, CDE-09, CDE-11

---

**XDQ-02 â Completeness of Aggregation Coverage (Population Reconciliation)**

- **Requirement intent:** The total population of exposure records contributing to an aggregated risk figure must be verifiably complete â every in-scope entity, business line, asset type, and geography must have contributed data, and the absence of data from any expected source must be flagged, not silently treated as zero.
- **Driven by:** Principle 4 (Â¶43) â "Supervisors expect banks to produce aggregated risk data that is complete and to measure and monitor the completeness of their risk data. Where risk data is not entirely complete, the impact should not be critical."
- **DQ dimensions:** completeness
- **Measurement:** Count of expected data sources (by entity, business line, and system) compared to count of sources that contributed records to the aggregation run; percentage of expected exposure-population represented in the aggregate; explicit flag and notional value for any source with zero records where non-zero is expected.
- **Suggested threshold:** 100% of expected sources represented; any source contributing zero records triggers an automated exception that must be resolved or documented before the report is issued.
- **CDEs spanned:** CDE-01, CDE-02, CDE-06, CDE-07, CDE-11

---

**XDQ-03 â Consistent Classification Across Source Systems**

- **Requirement intent:** Where the same instrument or counterparty is held or referenced across multiple booking systems, the key classification attributes â risk type, product type, business line, country, sector â must be consistently assigned. Divergence indicates either a data quality defect or an undocumented methodological choice; both require resolution before aggregation.
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (Â¶37) â "a bank should have a 'dictionary' of the concepts used, such that data is defined consistently across an organisation"; Principle 2 (Â¶33) â integrated data taxonomies and unified naming conventions.
- **DQ dimensions:** consistency, validity
- **Measurement:** For each instrument or counterparty appearing in more than one source system, the count and percentage of records where classification codes (risk type, product type, business line, sector, country) differ between systems, excluding documented approved overrides.
- **Suggested threshold:** Zero unexplained classification mismatches across source systems for any attribute designated as a CDE; documented override count tracked and reported to data owners quarterly.
- **CDEs spanned:** CDE-01, CDE-05, CDE-06, CDE-07, CDE-08, CDE-09

---

**XDQ-04 â Timeliness Compliance by Risk Type**

- **Requirement intent:** The elapsed time from position date close to availability of fully aggregated risk data in the reporting layer must be measured for each risk type and compared against the bank's defined SLAs, which must themselves be differentiated by risk type and scenario (normal vs. stress). The measurement must be automated and its output must itself be reportable to senior management.
- **Driven by:** Principle 5 (Â¶44â46) â timeliness requirements differentiated by risk type and scenario; Â¶47 â supervisory review of bank-specific frequency requirements.
- **DQ dimensions:** timeliness
- **Measurement:** For each risk type (credit, market, liquidity, operational), the measured elapsed time from position close to data availability in the aggregation layer; count of SLA breaches per period; trend over time.
- **Suggested threshold:** Zero SLA breaches for critical risk types in stress scenarios (Â¶46); defined breach tolerance for standard daily runs documented and approved by senior management.
- **CDEs spanned:** CDE-03, CDE-10, CDE-11, CDE-13

## 4. Out of Scope: What a Data Catalog Cannot Deliver

A data catalog â even one with a mature CDE register, DQ monitoring, lineage, and stewardship workflows â is an infrastructure for **knowing about data and measuring its quality**. BCBS 239 Principles 1 and 7â11 impose obligations that go beyond data knowledge into **governance behaviour, report design, and organisational accountability**. None of the following can be substituted by catalog configuration.

---

**Principle 1 (Â¶27â31): Board and senior management governance**

The regulation requires the board to approve the aggregation framework, understand its limitations, and receive reports that disclose those limitations. A catalog can document what limitations exist and make that documentation accessible â but it cannot ensure the board reads it, acts on it, or that senior management allocates resources in response. The obligation here is one of **documented institutional decision-making**, not metadata management. What is actually needed: board-approved data governance policies referencing the catalog's findings; documented senior management review of DQ exceptions; evidence trails in committee minutes.

---

**Principle 7 (Â¶52â56): Reconciliation and validation of risk reports**

Â¶53(a) requires defined processes to reconcile reports to risk data, and Â¶53(b) requires an inventory of validation rules applied to quantitative information. A catalog can hold that inventory and document the lineage from source to report â but it cannot execute the reconciliation, certify that it ran, or flag a report as unfit for distribution if it fails. That requires **operational report-production controls** (reconciliation workflows, exception management queues, report sign-off gates) in the reporting infrastructure, not in the catalog. What is actually needed: a reconciliation control framework with automated breaks reporting, escalation procedures, and a documented sign-off authority.

---

**Principle 8 (Â¶57â60): Comprehensiveness of risk report content**

The regulation requires that reports cover all material risk areas, include concentration information, forward-looking stress scenarios, and capital adequacy metrics. This is a **report design and content requirement**. A catalog can document which datasets feed which reports and flag when expected sources are missing â but it cannot determine whether the risk coverage in a report is adequate for the bank's actual risk profile, or whether forward-looking scenarios are sufficiently severe. What is actually needed: a reporting framework with defined content standards, owned by risk management and reviewed by the board, covering which risk types and metrics must appear in each report.

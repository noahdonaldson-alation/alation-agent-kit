## 1. Objectives and Scope

### What the regulation is trying to achieve

- **Prevent risk blindness at the top.** Boards and senior management must be able to rely on aggregated risk data to make critical decisions, including under stress. The failure mode the regulation targets is a bank that *has* the underlying data but cannot assemble it fast enough or accurately enough when it matters. (Â¶28: "board and senior management should review and approve the bank's group risk data aggregation and risk reporting framework"; Â¶52: "board and senior management can rely with confidence on the aggregated information to make critical decisions about risk.")

- **Require a single, governed data architecture â not a patchwork of spreadsheets.** Banks must establish integrated data taxonomies, metadata, single identifiers, and unified naming conventions across the group. Manual processes are explicitly a risk to be mitigated, not a default operating mode. (Â¶33: "establish integrated data taxonomies and architecture across the banking group, which includes information on the characteristics of the data (metadata), as well as use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions"; Â¶36(b): "where a bank relies on manual processes and desktop applicationsâ¦it should have effective mitigants in place.")

- **Demand measurable, monitored data quality â not assertions.** Accuracy, completeness, and timeliness are not compliance checkboxes; banks must *measure and monitor* them and have escalation channels when thresholds are breached. (Â¶40: "supervisors expect banks to measure and monitor the accuracy of data and to develop appropriate escalation channels and action plans"; Â¶43: "supervisors expect banks to produce aggregated risk data that is complete and to measure and monitor the completeness of their risk data.")

- **Ensure cross-entity and cross-risk aggregation is possible on demand.** Material risk exposures must be capturable across business lines, legal entities, asset types, industries, and geographies â including off-balance-sheet â and must be producible rapidly under stress, potentially intraday. (Â¶41: "risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures, including those that are off-balance sheet"; Â¶45: "banks need to build their risk systems to be capable of producing aggregated risk data rapidly during times of stress/crisis.")

- **Establish clear data ownership and lifecycle controls.** Business and IT owners, in partnership with risk managers, must maintain controls across the entire data lifecycle. (Â¶34: "roles and responsibilities should be established as they relate to the ownership and quality of risk data and information for both the business and IT functions.")

- **Require a data dictionary as a prerequisite.** Consistent concept definitions across the organisation are treated as a precondition for accuracy, not a nice-to-have. (Â¶37: "a bank should have a 'dictionary' of the concepts used, such that data is defined consistently across an organisation.")

### Who it applies to

BCBS 239 is addressed to **Global Systemically Important Banks (G-SIBs)** as its primary audience, with the Basel Committee explicitly noting an expectation that other internationally active banks should also meet the principles over time. The obligations fall on the **consolidated banking group** â meaning subsidiaries, branches, and material outsourced processes are in scope, not just the parent entity.

## 2. Critical Data Element Candidates

**CDE-01 â Counterparty Identifier**

- **Definition:** A unique, persistent identifier assigned to each legal entity or natural person that is a counterparty to the bank in any risk-bearing transaction. Unambiguous across business lines and geographies; does not change with relationship type.
- **Why critical:** Without a resolvable counterparty identifier, exposure records from different systems, business lines, and geographies cannot be joined into a single aggregate. Counterparty-level credit exposure â including concentration risk â cannot be computed. This is the single most common failure mode BCBS 239 is designed to address.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 â Its absence or inconsistency directly prevents any cross-system risk aggregation. Every other credit and counterparty CDE depends on it resolving correctly.
- **Driven by:** Principle 2 (Â¶33) â *"use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities, counterparties, customers and accounts"*; Principle 4 (Â¶41) â *"risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures"*
- **Search terms:** counterparty ID, counterparty reference, legal entity identifier, LEI, client ID, obligor ID, entity ID, party identifier, BIC, GIIN
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *uniqueness* â Each counterparty maps to exactly one authoritative identifier; no two distinct counterparties share an ID | count of duplicate counterparty identifier values across the master reference | target: 0 duplicates
  - *validity* â Every identifier resolves to a record in the authoritative counterparty master | count of exposure records carrying an identifier absent from the master reference | target: 0 unresolved
  - *completeness* â Every exposure record carries a populated counterparty identifier | rate of null or blank counterparty identifier across exposure records | target: 0% null
  - *consistency* â The same counterparty is represented by the same identifier regardless of originating system | count of cases where the same real-world entity carries different identifiers in different source systems | target: 0 cross-system mismatches after golden-record resolution

**CDE-02 â Legal Entity Identifier (of the bank's own booking entity)**

- **Definition:** The identifier of the bank's internal legal entity in which a transaction is booked â i.e., which subsidiary, branch, or holding company is the contracting party on the bank's side. Distinct from the counterparty identifier (CDE-01), which identifies the external party.
- **Why critical:** Aggregation across the banking group requires knowing which group entity carries each exposure. Without this, consolidation across subsidiaries is impossible and legal entityâlevel reporting (required for resolution planning and entity-level stress tests) breaks down.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, liquidity, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 â Required for both group-level aggregation and entity-level disaggregation. Missing or inconsistent booking-entity identifiers prevent any legal entity view.
- **Driven by:** Principle 2 (Â¶33) â *"single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities"*; Principle 4 (Â¶41) â *"capture and aggregate all material risk data across the banking group"*
- **Search terms:** booking entity, legal entity ID, entity code, branch code, subsidiary code, organisational unit, LEI (own entity), booking branch

**CDE-03 â Instrument / Facility Identifier**

- **Definition:** A unique identifier for each financial instrument, loan facility, contract, or position held by the bank. Serves as the primary join key between transactional systems, collateral systems, and risk engines.
- **Why critical:** Reconciliation between risk data and accounting data (Â¶36(c)) requires a common key at the instrument level. Without it, positions cannot be matched across systems, and the single authoritative source requirement (Â¶36(d)) cannot be enforced.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, counterparty, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 â Required for reconciliation and for assembling complete exposure per counterparty. Loss of this key breaks the chain between source transactions and risk aggregates.
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (Â¶36(c)) â *"risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate, to ensure that the risk data is accurate"*; Principle 3 (Â¶36(d)) â *"strive towards a single authoritative source for risk data per each type of risk"*
- **Search terms:** deal ID, trade ID, facility ID, contract ID, position ID, instrument ID, ISIN, CUSIP, account number, loan reference
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *uniqueness* â Each instrument has exactly one identifier in the authoritative source; no duplicates exist | count of duplicate instrument identifiers within a source system | target: 0 duplicates
  - *validity* â Every instrument identifier in the risk layer resolves to a record in the source system of record | count of risk records with unmatched instrument identifier | target: 0 unmatched
  - *consistency* â The same instrument carries the same identifier in the risk system and the general ledger | count of instruments present in both systems with mismatched identifiers | target: 0 cross-system mismatches

**CDE-04 â Gross Notional / Nominal Exposure Amount**

- **Definition:** The face value or notional principal of an exposure before the application of netting, collateral, or credit risk mitigation. Expressed in the transaction currency.
- **Why critical:** This is the raw input to all credit and counterparty exposure calculations. Errors in this figure flow directly into reported risk metrics, capital calculations, and limit monitoring. It is one of the fields that, if misstated, could constitute accounting materiality under Â¶56.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, concentration
- **Criticality:** 3 â Any inaccuracy here propagates into every downstream exposure figure and risk report.
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (Â¶36(a)) â *"controls surrounding risk data should be as robust as those applicable to accounting data"*; Principle 7 (Â¶56) â *"if omission or misstatement could influence the risk decisions of users, this may be considered material"*
- **Search terms:** notional amount, face value, principal amount, outstanding balance, drawn amount, nominal exposure, contract amount
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *accuracy* â The notional amount in the risk system agrees with the notional amount in the general ledger or system of record within a defined tolerance | sum of absolute differences between risk-system notional and GL notional, expressed as a percentage of total portfolio notional | target: â¤ 0.01% aggregate variance (or bank-defined materiality threshold)

  - *validity* â Notional amounts are positive numerics within a plausible range for the instrument type | count of records with negative or implausibly large notional values flagged for review | target: 0 unreviewed outliers

**CDE-05 â Transaction Currency**

- **Definition:** The ISO currency code in which the notional or nominal amount of a transaction is denominated.
- **Why critical:** Multi-currency aggregation requires consistent currency codes to apply exchange rates correctly. An incorrect or missing currency code produces silently wrong aggregated exposure figures â one of the harder errors to detect post-aggregation.
- **Risk types:** Market, credit, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 â Errors are silent and systemic: a currency miscoded as USD when it is EUR does not generate a null-field error; it generates a wrong number.
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (Â¶36(a)) â *"controls surrounding risk data should be as robust as those applicable to accounting data"*; Principle 4 (Â¶43) â *"supervisors expect banks' data to be materially complete, with any exceptions identified and explained"*

**CDE-06 â Risk Classification / Risk Type Code**

- **Definition:** The field(s) that classify an exposure or position by primary risk type (e.g., credit risk, market risk, liquidity risk, operational risk) and relevant sub-classification (e.g., retail vs. wholesale credit, trading book vs. banking book).

- **Risk types:** Cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 â Misclassification silently moves exposures into wrong aggregation buckets. The error is not visible in downstream figures unless reconciliation is performed.

**CDE-10 â Maturity / Tenor Date**

- **Definition:** The contractual maturity date or tenor end date of a loan, facility, bond, derivative, or other instrument. The date on which the principal obligation expires or the transaction terminates.

**CDE-11 â Collateral Value and Collateral Type**

- **Definition:** The estimated current value of collateral pledged against an exposure, and the type or asset class of that collateral (e.g., real estate, cash, securities). Distinct from the exposure notional.
- **Why critical:** Net credit exposure â what the bank is actually at risk of losing â requires subtracting recoverable collateral value from gross exposure. - **Risk types:** Credit, concentration
- **Criticality:** 2 â Collateral errors produce understatement of net credit risk. In a stress scenario, overvalued collateral is precisely the risk that materialises.
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (Â¶36(c)) â *"risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate"*; Principle 7 (Â¶56) â *"if omission or misstatement could influence the risk decisions of users, this may be considered material"*

**CDE-12 â Data-as-of Date / Snapshot Date**

- **Definition:** The business date as of which a position, exposure, or balance is recorded or valued. This is the reference date of the data, not the date of loading or processing.
- **Why critical:** Timeliness monitoring (Principle 5) and reconciliation between systems (Principle 3) both depend on knowing which date a data point represents. - **Risk types:** Cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 â The as-of date is a precondition for all time-based quality monitoring. Without it, timeliness SLAs cannot be measured and cross-system reconciliation is unreliable.
- **Driven by:** Principle 5 (Â¶44) â *"produce aggregate risk information on a timely basis to meet all risk management reporting requirements"*; Principle 3 (Â¶36(c)) â *"risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources"*
- **Search terms:** as-of date, snapshot date, value date, position date, reference date, business date, reporting date, effective date

### XDQ-01 â Risk-to-Finance Reconciliation

- **Description:** Risk exposure totals (gross notional, net exposure) must be reconciled to accounting balances in the general ledger at agreed frequencies and at defined levels of granularity (entity, product class, currency). ## 4. Out of Scope â What a Data Catalog Cannot Deliver

The following principles involve obligations that are fundamentally about **governance processes, reporting design, board behaviour, and organisational decision-making**. A data catalog â however well governed â cannot satisfy them, because they operate at a layer above data elements and data quality.

---

### Principle 8 â Comprehensiveness of risk reports (Â¶57â60)

The regulation requires that risk management reports cover all material risk areas, identify emerging concentrations, include forward-looking forecasts and stress-test results, and propose recommendations for action.

**What a catalog cannot do:** A catalog can document that a dataset exists and that its elements are populated and accurate. It cannot determine whether the *set of reports* covers all material risks, whether the scope of coverage is appropriate to the bank's risk profile, or whether forward-looking content is present. Report scope and coverage are decisions made by risk officers and approved by boards â not properties of data elements.

**What is needed instead:** A risk reporting framework document approved by the board (Â¶28, Â¶31), a report inventory that maps each report to the risk types and material risk areas it covers, and a periodic review process in which the board confirms coverage is adequate (Â¶59).

### Principle 9 â Clarity and usefulness of risk reports (Â¶61â69)

The regulation requires that reports be clear, concise, appropriately balanced between quantitative and qualitative content, and tailored to the needs of recipients at different levels of the organisation. **What a catalog cannot do:** Data quality monitoring confirms that data elements are accurate and complete. It cannot evaluate whether a report is clear, whether the balance between numbers and narrative is appropriate, whether a board member finds a dashboard comprehensible, or whether qualitative interpretation is adequate. These are judgements about presentation, communication, and governance â not data properties.

### Principle 10 â Frequency of report production and distribution (Â¶70â71)

The regulation requires that the board and senior management set frequency requirements for each report, that the bank routinely test its ability to produce reports within those timeframes, and that frequency increases under stress.

**What a catalog cannot do:** A catalog can document SLAs and measure data latency at the element level (CDE-12, XDQ-03). But it cannot set report-level frequency requirements, test end-to-end report production pipelines, or trigger the operational escalation that must occur when a stress scenario is declared. Report production cadence and stress-mode SLA testing are operational and governance matters.

**What is needed instead:** A report production inventory with documented normal and stress SLAs, owned by report operations. A testing programme for stress-scenario report production. An escalation protocol that defines what triggers stress-mode cadence and who authorises it. These may reference the catalog's timeliness measurements as inputs but are not themselves catalog functions.

**What is needed instead:** A report distribution framework with maintained recipient lists, access control and entitlements management (typically in identity and access management systems, not a catalog), and a periodic confirmation process â likely owned by risk reporting operations with sign-off from senior management.

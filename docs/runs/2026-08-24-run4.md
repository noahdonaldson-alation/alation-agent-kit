# BCBS 239: Data Catalog Requirements Analysis

---

## 1. Objectives and Scope

### What the regulation is trying to achieve

- **Reliable risk data aggregation under stress.** Banks must be able to produce accurate, complete, and timely aggregated risk data not just in normal operations but during crises â when decision speed is most critical. The framework exists because failures in risk data were a material contributor to the 2008 financial crisis. (Â¶35: *"risk data aggregation capabilities should meet all Principles below simultaneously"*; Â¶45: *"Banks need to build their risk systems to be capable of producing aggregated risk data rapidly during times of stress/crisis for all critical risks."*)

- **A single authoritative source of truth, not a patchwork of spreadsheets.** Principle 3 explicitly pushes banks toward automated, reconciled, single-source data and away from manual workarounds. (Â¶36(d): *"A bank should strive towards a single authoritative source for risk data per each type of risk."*)

- **Integrated data taxonomy and metadata, not siloed systems.** The regulation mandates a consistent data dictionary and unified naming conventions for entities, counterparties, customers, and accounts across the entire banking group. (Â¶33: *"A bank should establish integrated data taxonomies and architecture across the banking group, which includes information on the characteristics of the data (metadata), as well as use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions."*)

- **Owned, governed data with clear accountability.** Roles and responsibilities for data ownership â both business and IT â must be formally established for every element in the risk data lifecycle. (Â¶34: *"Roles and responsibilities should be established as they relate to the ownership and quality of risk data and information for both the business and IT functions."*)

- **Measured and monitored data quality, with escalation paths.** Accuracy and completeness are not assumed; they must be actively measured, with exceptions identified, explained, and escalated. (Â¶40: *"Supervisors expect banks to measure and monitor the accuracy of data and to develop appropriate escalation channels and action plans to be in place to rectify poor data quality."*; Â¶43: *"Supervisors expect banks to produce aggregated risk data that is complete and to measure and monitor the completeness of their risk data."*)

- **An inventory and classification of risk data items.** Principle 9 explicitly requires a catalogued reference linking data items to the concepts used in risk reports. (Â¶67: *"A bank should develop an inventory and classification of risk data items which includes a reference to the concepts used to elaborate the reports."*)

### Who and what it applies to

**Primary scope:** Globally Systemically Important Banks (G-SIBs) were required to comply by January 2016. The Basel Committee noted that national supervisors may extend requirements to Domestic SIBs (D-SIBs). In practice, most large internationally active banks treat this as applicable to their entire banking group.

**Organizational scope:** The entire banking group â all legal entities, subsidiaries, business lines, and geographies â not just the parent entity. Off-balance-sheet exposures are explicitly included (Â¶41).

**Data scope:** All material risk exposures across credit, market, liquidity, operational, and counterparty risk types. The word "material" is load-bearing: it is calibrated by the potential for omission or misstatement to influence risk decisions (Â¶56).

**CDE-01 â Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) / Internal Legal Entity Code**
- **Definition:** A standardized, unique code that unambiguously identifies a legal entity within the banking group or an external counterparty legal entity. May be the ISO 17442 LEI, an internal group entity code, or both maintained in a cross-reference.
- **Why critical:** Without a resolvable legal entity identifier, risk exposures cannot be aggregated across the group, subsidiaries cannot be included in consolidated reporting, and the bank cannot demonstrate group-wide coverage. It is the primary joining key for group consolidation.
- **Risk types:** Cross-cutting (credit, market, liquidity, concentration)
- **Criticality:** 3 â It is the foundational joining key for virtually every other aggregation. Its absence makes all cross-entity aggregation unreliable.
- **Driven by:** Principle 2 (Â¶33) â *"use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities, counterparties, customers and accounts"*; Principle 4 (Â¶41) â *"risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures"* across the banking group.
- **Search terms:** legal entity, LEI, entity code, group entity ID, subsidiary identifier, counterparty legal entity, entity hierarchy, GLEIF
- **Data quality requirements:**

  - *validity* â Every LEI used in risk data resolves to a record in the authoritative entity registry (internal or GLEIF) | count of LEI values in exposure records with no match in the entity reference | 0 unmatched
  - *completeness* â All subsidiaries in the group structure carry a legal entity identifier | percentage of entities in the group org chart without an assigned identifier | 0%
  - *timeliness* â Entity status (active/inactive/dissolved) reflects changes within the reporting cycle | age of last update to entity status field vs. current reporting date | â¤ 1 business day lag

**CDE-02 â Counterparty Identifier**
- **Definition:** A unique identifier that links all exposures â loans, derivatives, off-balance-sheet facilities, deposits â back to a single counterparty, regardless of which system, product line, or legal entity originated the exposure. - **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, concentration

**CDE-03 â Exposure Amount (Gross Notional / Outstanding Balance)**
- **Definition:** The monetary value representing a bank's gross financial exposure to a counterparty, instrument, or asset class at a given point in time. For loans, this is outstanding principal; for derivatives, gross notional or replacement cost as applicable to the risk measure in use.
- **Why critical:** It is the primary numeric quantity aggregated in virtually every risk report. Errors here directly distort exposure totals, concentration measures, and capital calculations. Principle 3 requires reconciliation to accounting sources (Â¶36(c)).
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, counterparty, concentration
- **Criticality:** 3 â The foundational measure for all quantitative risk aggregation. An accuracy failure here cannot be compensated for by any governance process.
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (Â¶36(a)) â *"Controls surrounding risk data should be as robust as those applicable to accounting data"*; Principle 3 (Â¶36(c)) â *"Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate."*
- **Search terms:** exposure amount, outstanding balance, notional amount, gross exposure, drawn balance, current exposure, replacement cost, mark-to-market exposure, carrying value
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *accuracy* â Aggregated exposure totals in risk systems reconcile to general ledger balances within an approved tolerance | variance between risk system aggregate and GL aggregate by entity and risk type | â¤ materiality threshold (typically < 0.1% or a fixed monetary tolerance set by policy)
  - *completeness* â No exposure record has a null or zero exposure amount where a non-zero balance exists in the source system | count of exposure records with null or unexplained zero balance | 0
  - *timeliness* â Exposure amounts reflect end-of-day or intraday positions consistent with the reporting cycle's cut-off | age of most recent position update vs. required cut-off time | within defined cut-off window
  - *validity* â Exposure amounts are denominated in a resolvable currency code and within plausible instrument-type ranges | count of records with invalid currency or amounts outside instrument-class bounds | 0

**CDE-04 â Risk Classification / Risk Type Code**
- **Definition:** A standardized code or taxonomy value that assigns each exposure or position to its primary risk type (credit, market, liquidity, operational, counterparty) and, within that, to a sub-type (e.g., corporate loan, traded derivative, off-balance-sheet guarantee). - **Why critical:** It is the classification that routes each exposure into the correct risk report. A miscoded exposure disappears from one risk bucket and inflates another, or is omitted from aggregation entirely. Principle 4 requires aggregation by risk type; Principle 7 requires reports to reflect risk accurately.
- **Risk types:** Cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 â Every risk report depends on correct classification. A systematic miscoding (e.g., off-balance-sheet items coded as on-balance-sheet) directly violates Â¶41.
- **Driven by:** Principle 4 (Â¶41) â *"risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures, including those that are off-balance sheet"*; Principle 7 (Â¶53(b)) â *"automated and manual edit and reasonableness checks, including an inventory of the validation rules that are applied to quantitative information."*
- **Search terms:** risk type, risk class, asset class, product type, instrument type, facility type, risk category, exposure type, off-balance-sheet flag, on/off balance sheet indicator
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *validity* â Every exposure record carries a risk classification value from the approved taxonomy; no free-text or null classifications in reportable positions | count of records with classification values not in the approved reference list | 0
  - *completeness* â Off-balance-sheet exposures are explicitly flagged and not omitted | count of instrument types known to generate off-balance-sheet exposure that carry no off-balance-sheet flag | 0
  - *consistency* â Risk classification for the same instrument type is applied consistently across business lines and legal entities | count of identical instrument types carrying different risk classifications across entities | 0

**CDE-05 â Business Line / Segment Code**

**CDE-09 â Maturity / Repricing Date**
- **Definition:** The contractual maturity date of a financial instrument, or the next repricing date for variable-rate instruments. For derivatives, the relevant tenor or expiry date.

- **Risk types:** Credit, liquidity
- **Criticality:** 2 â Critical for liquidity risk aggregation and time-bucketed credit risk reporting, but does not directly cause a counterparty identification failure.
- **Driven by:** Principle 5 (Â¶46(d)) â *"Liquidity risk indicators such as cash flows/settlements and funding"* are listed as critical risks requiring rapid aggregation; Principle 4 (Â¶41) requires all material risk exposures to be captured.

- **Data quality requirements:**

- **Definition:** A recorded link from each risk data element back to its authoritative source system, including the source system name, the extraction or feed identifier, and the date/time of the last feed. ## 3. Cross-Cutting Data Quality Requirements

These requirements span multiple CDEs and cannot be attached to any single element. They arise where the regulation demands systematic comparison across systems or domains.

---

### XCUT-01 â Risk-to-Finance Reconciliation

**What it is:** A formal reconciliation that ties aggregated risk exposure totals (CDE-03, Exposure Amount) back to general ledger balances by legal entity (CDE-01), product type (CDE-04), and business line (CDE-05).

**Regulatory basis:** Â¶36(a): *"Controls surrounding risk data should be as robust as those applicable to accounting data."* Â¶36(c): *"Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate."*

**DQ dimension:** *accuracy* and *consistency*

**Rule intent:** The sum of exposure amounts in the risk system, grouped by legal entity and risk type, reconciles to the corresponding GL balance within an approved materiality tolerance. Unexplained variances are surfaced as exceptions, assigned to an owner, and resolved before a report is signed off.

**Measurement:** Variance between risk system aggregate balance and GL balance, by legal entity Ã risk classification Ã reporting date. Count of variance items exceeding materiality threshold that remain unresolved at report sign-off time.

**Suggested threshold:** 0 unresolved material variances at report sign-off; all variances documented with root cause and remediation timeline.

**CDEs spanned:** CDE-01 (Legal Entity), CDE-03 (Exposure Amount), CDE-04 (Risk Classification), CDE-05 (Business Line), CDE-08 (Instrument ID)

---

### XCUT-02 â Cross-System Counterparty Identity Resolution

**What it is:** A check that a single counterparty (CDE-02) is represented by the same identifier across all source systems that contribute to risk aggregation â loan origination, derivatives booking, off-balance-sheet commitments, and collateral management. This is distinct from the uniqueness check on CDE-02 itself; it specifically tests cross-system consistency.

**Regulatory basis:** Â¶33: *"use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data includingâ¦counterparties"*; Â¶46: the bank must be able to aggregate total exposure to a single large corporate borrower rapidly, which requires all systems to resolve to the same counterparty ID.

**DQ dimension:** *consistency*

**Rule intent:** For any counterparty that has exposure recorded in more than one source system, all source systems reference the same master counterparty identifier. Fragmentation â where the same real-world counterparty appears under different IDs in different systems â results in an understatement of single-name concentration.

**Measurement:** Count of counterparties that can be matched by name/LEI across systems but carry different internal identifiers. Count of exposure records whose counterparty ID does not resolve to the counterparty master record.

**Suggested threshold:** 0 unmatched counterparty IDs across systems for material counterparties; < 0.5% for tail counterparties (subject to materiality policy).

**CDEs spanned:** CDE-01 (Legal Entity), CDE-02 (Counterparty ID), CDE-08 (Instrument ID)

---

### XCUT-03 â Completeness of Population Coverage

**What it is:** An aggregate check that the total population of in-scope exposures in the risk aggregation layer matches the population in all contributing source systems. This tests whether any positions have been silently dropped, excluded, or not yet onboarded to the risk aggregation infrastructure.

**Regulatory basis:** Â¶43: *"Supervisors expect banks to produce aggregated risk data that is complete and to measure and monitor the completeness of their risk data. Where risk data is not entirely complete, the impact should not be critical."* Â¶41 requires off-balance-sheet inclusion.

**DQ dimension:** *completeness*

**Rule intent:** The count and aggregate notional of positions in the risk aggregation system equals the count and aggregate notional in all source systems, for each risk type and legal entity. Any gap is documented as a known exclusion with a justified rationale and a remediation plan.

**Measurement:** (Count of positions in risk system) vs. (count of positions in source systems) by legal entity Ã risk type. Percentage of source-system notional not represented in the risk system. Count of known exclusions with no documented rationale.

**Suggested threshold:** 0% unexplained exclusion by notional value; all known exclusions documented with business owner sign-off.

**CDEs spanned:** CDE-01 (Legal Entity), CDE-03 (Exposure Amount), CDE-04 (Risk Classification), CDE-05 (Business Line), CDE-08 (Instrument ID), CDE-11 (Source System Reference)

---

### XCUT-04 â Timeliness of Feed Pipeline

**What it is:** An end-to-end check that data from each source system reaches the risk aggregation layer within the required cut-off window, and that the lag is monitored and alertable. This spans the feed infrastructure for all CDEs simultaneously.

**Regulatory basis:** Â¶44: *"A bank's risk data aggregation capabilities should ensure that it is able to produce aggregate risk information on a timely basis."* Â¶45 requires rapid production during stress/crisis for all critical risks. Â¶71 expects some intraday capability.

**DQ dimension:** *timeliness*

**Rule intent:** Every feed from every source system that contributes to a risk report completes within a documented maximum lag time. Feeds that miss their window are flagged automatically, escalated to the source system owner, and resolved before the report is generated.

**Measurement:** For each source system feed: actual feed completion timestamp minus required cut-off timestamp. Count of feed jobs completing after required cut-off, by risk type and reporting cycle. Maximum lag in minutes during stress-testing exercises.

**Suggested threshold:** 0 feeds completing after required cut-off without documented exception and escalation; stress/crisis intraday SLA defined per Principle 5 and tested at least annually.

**CDEs spanned:** CDE-03 (Exposure Amount), CDE-09 (Maturity Date), CDE-10 (Risk Measure Value), CDE-11 (Source System Reference) â and implicitly all other CDEs.

## 4. Out of Scope: What the Catalog Cannot Deliver

### Principle 1: Board and Senior Management Governance Accountabilities

**What the regulation requires (Â¶27â31):** The board must approve the risk data aggregation framework, understand its limitations, and be aware of compliance status. Senior management must deploy adequate resources and integrate risk data considerations into IT strategy and M&A due diligence.

**Why a catalog cannot deliver these:** A catalog can document framework coverage and flag gaps. These are governance and organizational decisions. The catalog provides evidence to support those decisions; it does not substitute for them.

### Principle 3: Independent Validation

**What the regulation requires (Â¶29(a)):** Risk data aggregation and reporting processes must be subject to independent validation by staff with specific IT, data, and reporting expertise. The validation must be integrated with other independent review activities.

**Why a catalog cannot deliver this:** A catalog can provide the inventory and DQ metrics that an independent validation team uses as evidence. The *act* of independent validation â assessing whether processes are functioning as intended, whether controls are adequate, whether the bank's implementation is appropriate for its risk profile â requires human judgment, audit methodology, and organizational independence. Catalog tooling supports validation; it does not constitute it.

### Principle 3: Manual Workaround Documentation and Reduction

**What the regulation requires (Â¶38â39):** Every manual process in the risk data aggregation chain must be documented with an explanation of its appropriateness and criticality, and the bank must have proposed actions to reduce its reliance on manual processes.

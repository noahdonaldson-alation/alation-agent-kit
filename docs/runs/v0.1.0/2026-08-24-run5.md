# BCBS 239 — Data Catalog Interpretation: What to Build and Govern

---

## 1. Objectives and Scope

### What the regulation is trying to achieve

- **Reliable risk aggregation as a precondition for safe governance.** The regulation requires that a bank can produce accurate, complete, and timely aggregated risk data — not only in normal conditions but under stress — so that boards and senior management can make decisions they can actually rely on. (¶35: *"risk management reports reflect the risks in a reliable way (ie meeting data aggregation expectations is necessary to meet reporting expectations)"*)

- **A single, authoritative, well-defined data layer for risk.** Banks must strive toward one authoritative source per risk type, supported by a "dictionary" of consistently defined concepts across the organisation. (¶36(d): *"A bank should strive towards a single authoritative source for risk data per each type of risk"*; ¶37: *"a bank should have a 'dictionary' of the concepts used, such that data is defined consistently across an organisation"*)

- **Integrated data architecture with explicit ownership.** Data taxonomies, metadata, single identifiers, and unified naming conventions must span the entire banking group. Business and IT owners must be assigned and accountable for data quality throughout the data lifecycle. (¶33: *"integrated data taxonomies and architecture across the banking group, which includes information on the characteristics of the data (metadata), as well as use of single identifiers"*; ¶34: *"Roles and responsibilities should be established as they relate to the ownership and quality of risk data"*)

- **Measurable, monitored, and escalated data quality.** Banks must not merely assert data quality — they must measure and monitor accuracy and completeness, maintain escalation channels, and have action plans for remediation. (¶40: *"banks to measure and monitor the accuracy of data and to develop appropriate escalation channels and action plans to be in place to rectify poor data quality"*; ¶43: *"produce aggregated risk data that is complete and to measure and monitor the completeness of their risk data"*)

- **Reconciliation between risk data and accounting data.** Risk data must be reconciled back to source systems including the general ledger, so that risk figures and financial figures cannot contradict each other without explanation. (¶36(c): *"Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate, to ensure that the risk data is accurate"*)

- **Adaptability for ad hoc, stress, and supervisory queries.** The data architecture must support on-demand slicing by business line, legal entity, asset type, industry, region, and other dimensions — not just pre-built reports. (¶50: *"banks to be able to generate subsets of data based on requested scenarios or resulting from economic events"*)

### Who it applies to

Principles 1–11 are directed at **Global Systemically Important Banks (G-SIBs)** as the primary addressees (the document was drafted in direct response to G-SIB weaknesses revealed in the 2007–2009 financial crisis), with an explicit expectation that supervisors will extend application to **other systemically important banks** over time. The principles apply at the **banking group level** — meaning consolidated entities, subsidiaries, and material legal entities — not merely at the parent institution. (¶29(b): *"any new initiatives, including acquisitions and/or divestitures, new product development, as well as broader process and IT change initiatives"* confirms group-wide scope.)

## 2. Critical Data Element Candidates

---

**CDE-01 — Counterparty Identifier**

- **Definition:** The unique, persistent identifier assigned to each legal counterparty across all systems in which that counterparty appears — trading, lending, derivatives, and deposits. Distinct from customer relationship IDs or account numbers; this identifies the legal entity that bears the exposure.
- **Why critical:** Without a stable, cross-system counterparty identifier, exposures cannot be summed across business lines or asset classes. A bank cannot know its total credit exposure to a single name — the most fundamental risk aggregation task.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 — Failure to resolve this identifier makes every aggregated credit and counterparty metric unreliable. It is the joining key for the majority of other CDEs.
- **Driven by:** Principle 2 (¶33) — *"use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities, counterparties, customers and accounts"*; Principle 4 (¶41) — *"risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures"*
- **Search terms:** counterparty ID, counterparty identifier, legal entity identifier, LEI, obligor ID, client ID, party ID, entity code, BIC, GIIN
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *uniqueness* — Each legal counterparty is represented by exactly one active identifier; no two records in the master file resolve to the same real-world legal entity under different codes | count of duplicate counterparty identifiers (same legal name, jurisdiction, and parent) carrying distinct codes | target: zero confirmed duplicates in production master
  - *validity* — Every counterparty identifier in an exposure record resolves to an active record in the counterparty master file | count of exposure records referencing a counterparty ID absent from or inactive in the master file | target: 0% unresolvable
  - *consistency* — The same counterparty identifier is used across all risk systems (credit, market, liquidity) without translation loss | count of identifier mismatches for the same counterparty when joined across system pairs | target: full cross-system concordance for all counterparties with material exposure

---

**CDE-02 — Legal Entity Identifier (Banking Group Hierarchy)**

- **Definition:** The identifier that places each operating entity, subsidiary, and branch within the bank's own group legal hierarchy — distinct from the counterparty identifier that identifies external parties. Must support roll-up from subsidiary to consolidated group level.
- **Why critical:** Aggregation across the banking group is only possible if every exposure, position, and balance can be assigned unambiguously to a node in the group legal structure. This is the primary dimension along which consolidation is performed.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, liquidity, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 — Principle 4 explicitly requires aggregation by legal entity; without this hierarchy, group-level reporting is impossible.
- **Driven by:** Principle 4 (¶41) — *"A bank's risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures, including those that are off-balance sheet"*; Principle 2 (¶33) — *"integrated data taxonomies and architecture across the banking group"*
- **Search terms:** legal entity code, entity ID, subsidiary code, branch code, group entity, consolidation entity, organisation unit, OU code, LEI (own entity), reporting entity
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *completeness* — Every exposure and position record carries a non-null legal entity identifier | count of records with null or blank legal entity field | target: 0% null
  - *validity* — Every legal entity identifier in transaction data resolves to a current, active node in the official group hierarchy | count of orphaned entity codes not present in the hierarchy master | target: zero orphans
  - *consistency* — The legal entity hierarchy used by risk systems matches the authoritative group structure registered with the board | count of nodes present in risk systems but absent from, or differently structured than, the board-approved hierarchy | target: zero discrepancies

---

**CDE-03 — Exposure Amount (Gross and Net)**

- **Definition:** The monetary value of the bank's risk exposure to a counterparty or instrument — both the gross amount before risk mitigation (collateral, netting, guarantees) and the net amount after recognised mitigants. Denominated in a stated currency.
- **Why critical:** This is the primary quantity being aggregated. All concentration limits, capital calculations, and large-exposure reports depend on the accuracy of this figure. Reconciliation to the general ledger anchors the entire risk data layer to the accounting record.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 — Inaccuracy here directly distorts every risk metric that uses it as an input.
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (¶36(a)) — *"Controls surrounding risk data should be as robust as those applicable to accounting data"*; Principle 3 (¶36(c)) — *"Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate"*
- **Search terms:** exposure amount, gross exposure, net exposure, outstanding balance, notional amount, drawn amount, EAD, exposure at default, carrying value, book value
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *accuracy* — Aggregated gross exposure by counterparty reconciles to the general ledger balance for the same counterparty within an agreed materiality tolerance | sum of risk exposure records vs. GL balance per counterparty; count and value of reconciling items outside tolerance | target: reconciling items below accounting materiality threshold (¶56 analogy)
  - *validity* — Exposure amounts are non-negative for long positions and flagged correctly for short or contingent positions; zero-value records are explicitly reviewed | count of records with implausible sign or value relative to product type | target: zero unexplained sign errors
  - *timeliness* — Exposure amounts reflect the position as of the stated valuation date with no stale records beyond the agreed settlement/booking lag | count of exposure records whose last-updated timestamp exceeds the agreed booking lag for that product type | target: defined per product type, consistent with Principle 5 requirements

---

**CDE-04 — Exposure Currency**

- **Definition:** The ISO currency code in which the exposure amount is denominated, before any translation to the reporting currency.
- **Why critical:** Without the source currency, FX translation to a common reporting currency cannot be verified or reproduced. Currency misattribution creates silent errors in aggregated figures that are invisible unless the source currency is auditable.
- **Risk types:** Market (FX), credit, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 — Secondary to exposure amount but required for accurate aggregation across currencies; errors here are amplified by FX volatility during stress.
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (¶36(c)) — *"Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate"*; Principle 4 (¶43) — *"produce aggregated risk data that is complete"*
- **Search terms:** currency code, ISO currency, denomination currency, trade currency, CCY, FX code, base currency
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *validity* — Every exposure record carries a valid ISO 4217 currency code | count of records with null, blank, or non-ISO currency codes | target: 0% invalid
  - *consistency* — The currency code on a risk exposure record matches the currency on the corresponding general ledger entry for the same instrument | count of currency-code mismatches on matched GL/risk record pairs | target: zero mismatches

---

**CDE-05 — Product / Instrument Type**

- **Definition:** A controlled vocabulary classification of the financial instrument giving rise to the exposure — for example, term loan, revolving credit, OTC derivative, repo, bond, guarantee. This is a taxonomy-level classification, not a free-text description.
- **Why critical:** Product type controls which risk calculation methodology applies (e.g. SA-CCR for derivatives vs. standardised approach for loans), drives capital treatment, and determines which aggregation logic is appropriate. It is also the primary basis for ensuring off-balance-sheet items are captured (¶41).
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, counterparty, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 — Misclassification routes an exposure through the wrong risk engine; the error may not surface until stressed conditions expose the wrong capital treatment.
- **Driven by:** Principle 4 (¶41) — *"risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures, including those that are off-balance sheet"*; Principle 2 (¶33) — *"integrated data taxonomies and architecture"*
- **Search terms:** product type, instrument type, asset class, product code, instrument class, product category, asset type, transaction type
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *validity* — Every exposure record carries a product type drawn from the bank's approved taxonomy; no free-text, null, or legacy codes outside the taxonomy | count of records with product types not present in the approved taxonomy | target: 0% non-conforming
  - *completeness* — Off-balance-sheet instruments (guarantees, commitments, derivatives) are present in the risk data set at rates consistent with the accounting disclosure of contingent liabilities | ratio of off-balance-sheet risk records to disclosed off-balance-sheet notional in financial statements | target: no unexplained gap exceeding materiality threshold
  - *consistency* — The product type classification on the risk record matches the product type on the corresponding accounting entry | count of classification mismatches on matched pairs | target: zero mismatches

---

**CDE-06 — Risk Classification / Risk Category**

- **Definition:** The assignment of an exposure or position to a regulatory and internal risk category — credit risk, market risk, liquidity risk, operational risk, counterparty credit risk — for purposes of capital calculation and risk reporting. Where sub-classifications exist (e.g. credit risk sub-type: sovereign, corporate, retail), those sub-classes are also in scope.
- **Why critical:** Reports to the board and to supervisors are structured by risk category (¶57). Capital adequacy calculations depend on correct category assignment. An exposure in the wrong risk bucket receives wrong capital treatment and distorts all category-level aggregates.
- **Risk types:** Cross-cutting (classification drives all other risk types)
- **Criticality:** 3 — Misclassification has direct capital and regulatory reporting consequences.
- **Driven by:** Principle 7 (¶53(a)) — *"Defined requirements and processes to reconcile reports to risk data"*; Principle 8 (¶57) — *"exposure and position information for all significant risk areas (eg credit risk, market risk, liquidity risk, operational risk)"*
- **Search terms:** risk type, risk category, risk class, Basel asset class, regulatory asset class, risk bucket, risk sub-type, exposure class
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *validity* — Every exposure record carries a risk category drawn from the bank's approved risk taxonomy | count of records with null or non-standard risk categories | target: 0% non-conforming
  - *consistency* — Risk category assigned in the risk system is consistent with the regulatory capital treatment applied to the same record in the capital calculation engine | count of records where risk category and capital treatment are logically inconsistent | target: zero logical inconsistencies
  - *accuracy* — Aggregated exposure by risk category in risk reports ties back to the underlying record-level data without unexplained residuals | value of reconciling items between risk report totals and summed record-level data by category | target: zero unexplained residuals

---

**CDE-07 — Business Line**

- **Definition:** The internal business line or division to which an exposure or position is attributed for management reporting — for example, corporate banking, retail banking, trading, private banking, treasury. Must map to a controlled list approved for group-level aggregation.
- **Why critical:** Principle 4 explicitly requires data to be available by business line. Without clean business-line attribution, the bank cannot aggregate or compare risk across divisions, cannot identify concentrations within a line, and cannot produce business-line-level risk reports.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, liquidity, operational, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 — A necessary aggregation dimension, but typically derivable from other attributes if the primary attribution is missing; criticality is high but slightly lower than counterparty or exposure amount.
- **Driven by:** Principle 4 (¶41–43) — *"Data should be available by business line, legal entity, asset type, industry, region and other groupings"* (from the Principle 4 header statement); Principle 6 (¶50) — *"aggregate risk data quickly on country credit exposures … across all business lines and geographic areas"*
- **Search terms:** business line, business unit, division, desk, segment, LOB, line of business, cost centre (where used as business-line proxy), profit centre
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *completeness* — Every exposure and position record carries a non-null business line code | count of records with null or blank business line | target: 0% null
  - *validity* — Business line codes reference only entries in the approved business line taxonomy | count of orphaned business line codes not in the approved list | target: zero orphans
  - *consistency* — The business line attributed to an exposure in the risk system matches the business line on the corresponding P&L or cost centre record in the management accounts | count of attribution mismatches on matched pairs | target: below agreed materiality threshold

---

**CDE-08 — Geography / Jurisdiction**

- **Definition:** The country or jurisdiction code associated with an exposure — specifically the country of the counterparty's primary risk (not necessarily booking location), assigned using a controlled vocabulary (ISO 3166 or equivalent). Where relevant, both country of risk and country of booking should be separately maintained.
- **Why critical:** Aggregation by country is one of the most common stress-scenario requests (¶50 gives the example explicitly). Country concentration limits depend on this field. Cross-border data-sharing restrictions referenced in ¶30 also depend on correct jurisdiction tagging.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 — Essential for geographic aggregation and stress scenarios; errors become critical during country-specific stress events.
- **Driven by:** Principle 6 (¶50) — *"aggregate risk data quickly on country credit exposures as of a specified date based on a list of countries"*; Principle 4 (¶41) — completeness across all material dimensions including geography
- **Search terms:** country code, country of risk, country of domicile, jurisdiction, ISO country code, booking country, risk country, geographic region
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *validity* — Every exposure record carries a country code conforming to ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 or the bank's approved equivalent | count of records with null, blank, or non-ISO country codes | target: 0% invalid
  - *completeness* — Both country of risk and country of booking are populated where product type warrants the distinction | count of records of applicable product types missing either country field | target: 0% missing on applicable types
  - *consistency* — Country of risk attribution is consistent with the counterparty master file entry for that counterparty's domicile jurisdiction | count of mismatches between exposure-level country-of-risk and counterparty master domicile | target: zero unexplained mismatches

---

**CDE-09 — Industry / Sector Classification**

- **Definition:** The economic sector or industry to which a counterparty or borrower belongs, assigned using a controlled classification system (NACE, GICS, SIC, or internal equivalent mapped to one of these). Applied at counterparty level, not transaction level.
- **Why critical:** Sector concentration is an explicit reporting requirement (¶57: *"single name, country and industry sector for credit risk"*). Without clean sector data, the bank cannot identify or monitor sectoral concentrations that may signal emerging systemic risk.
- **Risk types:** Credit, concentration
- **Criticality:** 2 — Required for concentration reporting; errors lead to silent under-reporting of sector concentrations.
- **Driven by:** Principle 8 (¶57) — *"significant components of those risk areas (eg single name, country and industry sector for credit risk)"*; Principle 6 (¶50) — *"industry credit exposures as of a specified date based on a list of industry types across all business lines"*
- **Search terms:** industry code, sector code, NACE code, GICS sector, SIC code, industry classification, counterparty sector, borrower industry
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *completeness* — Every credit counterparty with a material exposure carries a non-null industry classification | count of counterparties with material exposure and null industry code | target: 0% null for material counterparties
  - *validity* — All industry codes reference an approved classification system; no free-text entries | count of industry codes not resolving to an entry in the approved taxonomy | target: 0% non-conforming
  - *consistency* — Where the same counterparty appears across multiple business lines or systems, the same industry classification is applied | count of counterparties with conflicting industry codes across system instances | target: zero conflicts in counterparty master

---

**CDE-10 — Valuation Date / Position Date**

- **Definition:** The specific date as of which an exposure amount, position, or risk metric is stated. This is the reference date of the data record, not the trade date or the report-generation date.
- **Why critical:** Aggregation across records is only valid if all records share or are correctly adjusted to a common valuation date. Stale records mixed with current records produce a distorted aggregate. Principle 5 requires aggregation to be up to date; the valuation date is the field that makes "up to date" measurable.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, liquidity, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 — A metadata field, but one whose miscapture or inconsistency makes every aggregated figure suspect.
- **Driven by:** Principle 5 (¶44) — *"risk data aggregation capabilities should ensure that it is able to produce aggregate risk information on a timely basis"*; Principle 7 (¶53(a)) — *"Defined requirements and processes to reconcile reports to risk data"* (reconciliation requires a common reference date)
- **Search terms:** valuation date, position date, as-of date, reference date, snapshot date, report date, trade date vs settlement date, effective date
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *timeliness* — The valuation date on each record does not lag the agreed cut-off for that product type by more than the permitted booking lag | count of records whose valuation date is older than the permitted lag relative to the aggregation run date | target: 0% stale beyond permitted lag, with stricter tolerance for trading and liquidity risk
  - *validity* — Valuation dates are non-null and represent a real calendar date within an expected operating range (not future-dated, not implausibly old) | count of records with null, future, or implausibly dated valuation date | target: 0% invalid
  - *consistency* — Within a single aggregation run, all records contributing to the same risk metric share the same valuation date or are explicitly adjusted to a common date with the adjustment documented | count of records in the same aggregation cohort carrying different valuation dates without documented adjustment | target: zero unexplained date inconsistencies within a cohort

---

**CDE-11 — Risk Limit and Risk Appetite Reference**

- **Definition:** The approved limit or risk appetite threshold against which a risk exposure or metric is to be measured — for example, a single-name credit limit, a country limit, a VaR limit, a liquidity coverage ratio floor. Includes the limit value, the metric it applies to, the organisational level at which it is set, and its effective date.
- **Why critical:** Principle 8 explicitly requires reports to provide *"information in the context of limits and risk appetite/tolerance"* (¶58). A risk figure is only interpretable in context of its limit. If limit data is absent or stale, the board cannot assess whether the bank is within appetite — which is the purpose of risk reporting.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, liquidity, operational, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 — Technically a reference/metadata element, but its absence or staleness directly impairs the board's ability to act on risk reports.
- **Driven by:** Principle 8 (¶58) — *"provide information in the context of limits and risk appetite/tolerance and propose recommendations for action where appropriate"*; Principle 9 (¶64) — *"allow it to fulfil its governance mandate … ensure it is operating within its risk tolerance/appetite"*
- **Search terms:** risk limit, risk appetite, limit value, risk threshold, VaR limit, concentration limit, single-name limit, country limit, liquidity limit, LCR floor, NSFR floor, limit register
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *timeliness* — Limit values in the risk reporting data set reflect board- or management-approved limits as of the current reporting period; superseded limits are retired | count of limit records whose approval date is older than the most recent board or committee review cycle | target: zero limits not refreshed within the approved review cycle
  - *completeness* — A limit record exists for every material risk metric for which the board or senior management has set a threshold | count of risk metrics reported to the board without a corresponding limit or appetite reference | target: zero unreferenced material metrics
  - *accuracy* — Limit values in the risk system match the values in the board-approved limit register without manual translation error | count of discrepancies between system limit values and the authoritative limit register | target: zero discrepancies

---

**CDE-12 — Collateral / Credit Risk Mitigation Identifier and Value**

- **Definition:** The identifier and current market value of collateral or other credit risk mitigants (guarantees, credit derivatives, netting agreements) recognised against a specific exposure. Includes the mitigant type, the collateral value, the haircut applied, and the link to the gross exposure it mitigates.
- **Why critical:** The difference between gross and net exposure (CDE-03) depends entirely on the completeness and accuracy of collateral records. Under-recorded collateral over-states net exposure; over-recorded or stale collateral under-states it. Both distort capital calculations and concentration metrics.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty
- **Criticality:** 2 — Directly affects net exposure accuracy and capital adequacy; errors are particularly dangerous because they may not be visible in gross-exposure monitoring.
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (¶36(a)) — *"Controls surrounding risk data should be as robust as those applicable to accounting data"*; Principle 4 (¶41) — *"include all material risk exposures, including those that are off-balance sheet"* (collateral received is off-balance-sheet for the bank)
- **Search terms:** collateral ID, collateral value, haircut, collateral type, security interest, pledge, netting agreement, ISDA master, CSA, guarantee, credit protection, eligible collateral
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *timeliness* — Collateral values reflect market prices as of the most recent valuation date consistent with the collateral agreement terms; stale valuations are flagged | count of collateral records whose valuation date exceeds the contractual revaluation frequency | target: 0% stale beyond contractual frequency
  - *completeness* — Every collateral agreement associated with an exposure record is linked in the risk data | count of exposure records with documented collateral agreements in the legal register that have no corresponding collateral record in the risk system | target: zero unlinked agreements
  - *accuracy* — Aggregated net exposure (gross minus recognised collateral) reconciles to the accounting net carrying value for the same instrument | value of reconciling items between risk-system net exposure and GL net carrying value | target: within accounting materiality threshold

## 3. Cross-Cutting Data Quality Requirements

### XCDQ-01 — Risk-to-Finance Reconciliation

**Purpose:** Principle 3 (¶36(c)) requires risk data to be reconciled to accounting sources. This is not a single-CDE requirement — it spans the entire relationship between the risk data layer and the general ledger.

**Scope:** Spans CDE-01 (Counterparty Identifier), CDE-02 (Legal Entity), CDE-03 (Exposure Amount), CDE-04 (Currency), CDE-05 (Product Type), CDE-12 (Collateral Value)

**Rule intent:** For every material risk category and legal entity, the sum of gross exposures in the risk system should agree to the corresponding balance in the general ledger within an agreed materiality tolerance. Reconciling items must be individually identified, explained, and tracked to resolution.

**Measurement:** Total unexplained reconciling value between risk aggregation and GL by legal entity, risk category, and reporting period; count of line items in the reconciliation with no assigned explanation; age of unresolved reconciling items.

**Suggested threshold:** No unexplained reconciling item exceeding the bank's accounting materiality threshold (applying ¶56's analogy to accounting materiality) for more than the agreed resolution period.

**Driven by:** Principle 3 (¶36(a),(c)) — *"Controls surrounding risk data should be as robust as those applicable to accounting data… reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate"*

---

### XCDQ-02 — Cross-System Counterparty Identity Resolution

**Purpose:** A counterparty may appear under different internal codes across trading, lending, derivatives, and deposits systems. Without a resolved identity layer, aggregation across systems double-counts or misses exposure. This is a prerequisite for CDE-01 to function.

**Scope:** Spans CDE-01 (Counterparty Identifier), CDE-02 (Legal Entity), CDE-03 (Exposure Amount), CDE-07 (Business Line)

**Rule intent:** Every counterparty appearing in any risk-relevant system has a resolved mapping to a single entry in the authoritative counterparty master, and that mapping is maintained as systems evolve.

**Measurement:** Count of counterparty identifiers in source systems with no mapping to the master file; count of mappings flagged as unverified or expired; count of counterparties with material exposure whose master record has not been reviewed within the agreed periodic review cycle.

**Suggested threshold:** Zero unmapped counterparties with material exposure; 100% of mappings verified at least annually.

**Driven by:** Principle 2 (¶33) — *"use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities, counterparties"*

---

### XCDQ-03 — Group Hierarchy Completeness and Consistency

**Purpose:** Aggregation from subsidiary to group level requires the legal entity hierarchy to be complete and consistent across all systems that contribute data to group risk reports. A subsidiary missing from one system will be silently excluded from group totals.

**Scope:** Spans CDE-02 (Legal Entity), CDE-03 (Exposure Amount), CDE-07 (Business Line), CDE-08 (Geography)

**Rule intent:** The set of legal entities contributing data to risk aggregation is complete relative to the bank's official group structure; no material subsidiary, branch, or special-purpose entity is absent. The hierarchy version used in risk aggregation is the same version approved by the board.

**Measurement:** Count of entities in the official group structure with no corresponding data contribution to risk aggregation; count of entities present in risk systems that are absent from the official structure; date of last reconciliation between the risk hierarchy and the board-approved structure.

**Suggested threshold:** Zero material entities absent from risk aggregation; hierarchy reconciled at least at every reporting period end and within five business days of any group structural change.

**Driven by:** Principle 4 (¶43) — *"produce aggregated risk data that is complete and to measure and monitor the completeness of their risk data"*; Principle 2 (¶33) — *"integrated data taxonomies and architecture across the banking group"*

---

### XCDQ-04 — Timeliness SLA Monitoring Across Risk Types

**Purpose:** Principle 5 requires different data to be available at different speeds, with tighter requirements during stress. A single SLA framework covering all CDEs by risk type and scenario (normal vs. stress) is needed.

**Scope:** Spans CDE-03 (Exposure Amount), CDE-10 (Valuation Date), CDE-11 (Limit Reference), and all market and liquidity CDEs

**Rule intent:** Every risk data set that feeds a time-sensitive report (trading positions, liquidity indicators, large counterparty exposures) is available within the bank's stated SLA for that data type, in both normal and stress conditions. SLA performance is logged and trended.

**Measurement:** Count of reporting runs where data delivery exceeded the stated SLA, by risk type and scenario; percentage of stress-scenario report runs completed within the stress-mode SLA target; trend of SLA breaches over rolling periods.

**Suggested threshold:** Zero breaches of stress-mode SLA for critical risks identified in ¶46 (large corporate credit, CCR/derivatives, trading positions, liquidity, time-critical operational indicators); normal-mode SLA breach rate below agreed operational threshold.

**Driven by:** Principle 5 (¶45) — *"banks need to build their risk systems to be capable of producing aggregated risk data rapidly during times of stress/crisis for all critical risks"*

## 4. Out of Scope — What a Data Catalog Cannot Deliver

The following requirements arise from Principles 8–11 and from governance obligations within Principles 1–7. They are real BCBS 239 obligations. A data catalog and CDE/DQ framework address the data layer — they do not address what follows.

### Principle 8 — Comprehensiveness of report content (¶57–60)

¶57–60 require that risk reports cover all material risk areas, include forward-looking forecasts and stress test results, identify emerging concentrations, and contextualise metrics against limits and appetite. These are **report design and editorial** requirements. A data catalog can confirm that the underlying data elements are present, complete, and of known quality. It cannot determine whether the *selection* of content in a board report is appropriate for the bank's risk profile, whether the *balance* between quantitative data and qualitative interpretation is right, or whether forecasts are adequately forward-looking. That requires a reporting governance framework — report templates, content standards, and a formal board review of reporting adequacy — sitting outside the catalog.

### Principle 9 — Clarity, usefulness, and recipient tailoring (¶61–69)

¶61–69 require that reports are clear, concise, and tailored to recipients; that the board actively assesses whether it is receiving the right information; and that the bank periodically confirms with recipients that reports remain relevant and appropriate. These are **communication design and board engagement** requirements. A data catalog cannot assess whether a report is comprehensible to a non-technical board member, whether the level of aggregation is appropriate for a given recipient, or whether the board is actually engaging with and challenging the information it receives. Satisfying ¶69 (*"confirm periodically with recipients that the information aggregated and reported is relevant and appropriate"*) requires a documented board/management feedback process — a governance activity, not a data management one.

### Principle 10 — Frequency-setting and stress-testing of report production (¶70–71)

¶70–71 require the board and senior management to *set* frequency requirements for each report under normal and stress conditions, and require the bank to routinely *test* its ability to produce accurate reports within those timeframes, particularly under stress. The data catalog can expose timeliness SLA metadata (XCDQ-04) and flag when data arrives late. It cannot enforce board decisions about what frequency is appropriate, and it cannot run the operational rehearsal exercises that ¶70 (*"routinely test its ability to produce accurate reports within established timeframes"*) demands. Stress-testing of reporting production requires a business continuity and operational resilience program.

### Principle 11 — Distribution controls and confidentiality (¶72–73)

¶72–73 require procedures for rapid, confidential dissemination of reports to appropriate recipients, with periodic confirmation that recipients receive timely reports. Access controls on catalog assets — business glossary entries, data set profiles, lineage — are entirely separate from the report distribution controls that govern who receives a finished risk report and through which channel. Report distribution is an entitlement management and secure delivery problem, typically governed by the risk reporting system, not the data catalog. The catalog can document sensitivity classifications on data elements, but it cannot enforce report delivery controls.

### Principle 1 — Independent validation of aggregation and reporting processes (¶29(a))

¶29(a) requires *independent* validation that aggregation and reporting processes function as intended and are appropriate to the bank's risk profile. This must be conducted by staff with specific IT, data, and reporting expertise. A data catalog populated and governed by the data management or risk teams cannot self-certify. The independent validation requirement means a separate function — internal audit, model validation, or an equivalent independent review team — must assess the catalog's own completeness and the DQ monitoring it contains. The catalog is an input to that validation, not a substitute for it.

### Principle 1 — Governance framework, board approval, and resource allocation (¶27–28, 30–31)

The overarching governance obligations — board approval of the aggregation framework, senior management awareness of coverage limitations, IT strategic planning to remediate gaps, adequate financial and human resource allocation — are organisational and board-level commitments. A data catalog can provide evidence of data quality status and coverage gaps that informs those decisions. It cannot replace the decision-making, the resource allocation, or the board oversight itself.

# BCBS 239 — Data Catalog Interpretation: What to Build and Govern

---

## 1. Objectives and Scope

### What the regulation is trying to achieve

- **Reliable risk aggregation under stress.** Banks must produce accurate, complete, and timely aggregate risk data not only in normal conditions but during crises — when data failures are most consequential. (¶35: *"risk data aggregation capabilities should meet all Principles below simultaneously"*; ¶45: *"Banks need to build their risk systems to be capable of producing aggregated risk data rapidly during times of stress/crisis for all critical risks."*)

- **A single, authoritative, well-defined data foundation.** Risk data must be reconciled to accounting sources, defined consistently through a shared dictionary, and sourced from a single authoritative system per risk type. (¶36(c)(d): *"Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate"* and *"a bank should strive towards a single authoritative source for risk data per each type of risk."*)

- **Group-wide completeness across all material dimensions.** Aggregation must span all legal entities, business lines, asset types, geographies, and off-balance-sheet items — with any gaps identified, explained, and monitored. (¶41: *"A bank's risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures, including those that are off-balance sheet."*; ¶43: *"any exceptions identified and explained."*)

- **Integrated data architecture with formal ownership.** Banks must establish unified taxonomies, metadata, single identifiers, and named data owners for both business and IT functions, with controls across the data lifecycle. (¶33: *"A bank should establish integrated data taxonomies and architecture across the banking group, which includes information on the characteristics of the data (metadata), as well as use of single identifiers."*; ¶34: *"Roles and responsibilities should be established as they relate to the ownership and quality of risk data."*)

- **Governed, validated, and documented processes.** All aggregation processes — automated or manual — must be documented, subject to independent validation, and supported by escalation channels when data quality degrades. (¶29(a): *"Fully documented and subject to high standards of validation… independent and review the bank's compliance."*; ¶40: *"banks to measure and monitor the accuracy of data and to develop appropriate escalation channels."*)

- **Risk reporting that enables board-level decision-making.** Reports must be accurate, comprehensive, clear, timely, and appropriately distributed so that boards and senior management can act on risk information. (¶52: *"Risk management reports should be accurate and precise to ensure a bank's board and senior management can rely with confidence on the aggregated information."*)

### Who it applies to

BCBS 239 is directed at **Globally Systemically Important Banks (G-SIBs)** — the primary addressees at time of publication — and is broadly expected by supervisors to inform practice at all large, internationally active banks. The obligations fall on the **bank as an institution**: its board, senior management, risk function, IT function, and data owners across the banking group, including subsidiaries and, where relevant, outsourced processes (¶27).

## 2. Critical Data Element Candidates

---

**CDE-01 — Counterparty Identifier**

- **Definition:** A unique, persistent code that identifies a single legal counterparty across all systems in which the bank holds an exposure to that counterparty — including loans, derivatives, securities, and contingent instruments.
- **Why critical:** Without a resolvable counterparty identifier, exposures from different systems cannot be joined to produce a single aggregate exposure. This is the primary joining key for credit risk aggregation and single-name concentration reporting.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, concentration
- **Criticality:** 3 — Its absence makes cross-system credit aggregation structurally impossible. No other compensating control can substitute at scale.
- **Driven by:** Principle 2 (¶33) — *"use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities, counterparties, customers and accounts"*; Principle 4 (¶41) — *"risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures."*
- **Search terms:** counterparty ID, counterparty code, legal entity identifier, LEI, obligor ID, client ID, party ID, GFCID, golden source party
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *uniqueness* — Each real-world counterparty maps to exactly one identifier in the authoritative source | count of counterparty identifiers that resolve to more than one distinct legal entity record | target: 0
  - *completeness* — Every exposure record carries a populated counterparty identifier | count of exposure records with null or blank counterparty identifier | target: 0 for credit and counterparty risk books
  - *validity* — Every counterparty identifier in a risk system resolves to a record in the authoritative counterparty master | count of identifiers present in exposure systems but absent from the counterparty master | target: 0
  - *consistency* — The same counterparty identifier is used for the same legal counterparty across all risk systems (credit, market, operations) | count of cross-system exposure records for the same counterparty that carry non-matching identifiers | target: 0

---

**CDE-02 — Legal Entity Identifier (Internal and/or LEI)**

- **Definition:** A code that uniquely identifies the legal entity within the banking group that owns or books a risk exposure — distinct from the counterparty identifier, which identifies the external obligor. Also encompasses the external LEI where used for counterparties subject to LEI registration.
- **Why critical:** Group-wide aggregation depends on knowing which legal entity within the bank holds each exposure. Without it, cross-entity consolidation cannot be performed and regulatory capital cannot be correctly allocated. It is also a required grouping dimension under Principle 4.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, liquidity, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 — All group-level aggregation and consolidation depends on this element being populated and consistent.
- **Driven by:** Principle 4 (¶41) — *"A bank's risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures, including those that are off-balance sheet"*; Principle 2 (¶33) — *"single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities."*
- **Search terms:** legal entity code, booking entity, booking entity ID, entity code, LEI, MFI code, subsidiary code, reporting entity, consolidated entity
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *completeness* — Every exposure and position record carries a populated legal entity identifier | count of records with null legal entity identifier | target: 0
  - *validity* — Every legal entity identifier resolves to an active entry in the group's authoritative entity hierarchy | count of identifiers not present in the entity master | target: 0
  - *consistency* — The legal entity identifier used in risk systems matches the identifier used in the general ledger for the same booking unit | count of mismatches between risk system and GL entity codes for the same transaction population | target: 0

---

**CDE-03 — Exposure Amount / Nominal Value**

- **Definition:** The monetary value representing a bank's financial exposure to a counterparty or instrument at a given point in time — expressed in the currency of the instrument before any conversion. This includes on-balance-sheet outstanding balances, notional amounts for derivatives, and committed undrawn amounts for off-balance-sheet facilities.
- **Why critical:** It is the primary input to credit exposure aggregation, capital calculations, and limit monitoring. Errors here directly distort every downstream risk figure.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, concentration
- **Criticality:** 3 — Every material risk report contains an exposure figure derived from this element. Misstatement is directly analogous to accounting materiality (¶56).
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (¶36(a)) — *"Controls surrounding risk data should be as robust as those applicable to accounting data"*; Principle 7 (¶56) — *"if omission or misstatement could influence the risk decisions of users, this may be considered material."*
- **Search terms:** outstanding balance, drawn amount, nominal amount, notional, committed exposure, EAD, exposure at default, carrying value, book value, facility outstandings
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *accuracy* — The exposure amount in risk systems reconciles to the corresponding balance in the general ledger within an agreed tolerance | sum of absolute differences between risk system exposure and GL balance for matched instruments | threshold: defined by the bank's materiality standard, analogous to accounting materiality
  - *completeness* — Every booked instrument has a non-null exposure amount | count of instrument records with null or zero exposure where the instrument is live and not intentionally unfunded | target: 0
  - *timeliness* — Exposure amounts reflect the position as of the required reporting reference date | count of exposure records whose as-of date lags the reporting reference date beyond the agreed refresh cycle | target: 0 at the point of report generation
  - *validity* — Exposure amounts are denominated in a recognised currency code and are non-negative for drawn balances | count of records with unrecognised currency codes or negative drawn balances | target: 0

---

**CDE-04 — Instrument / Facility Identifier**

- **Definition:** A unique code that identifies a specific credit facility, security, derivative contract, or other financial instrument — the lowest level at which an individual exposure is recorded and managed.
- **Why critical:** It is the joining key between source transaction systems and risk aggregation. Without a stable instrument identifier, the same exposure may be counted multiple times or omitted from aggregation entirely, directly corrupting completeness.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, counterparty, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 — It is the atomic unit of aggregation. Deduplication, reconciliation, and cross-system joins all depend on it.
- **Driven by:** Principle 2 (¶33) — *"single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including… accounts"*; Principle 3 (¶36(c)) — *"Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources."*
- **Search terms:** deal ID, facility ID, contract number, trade ID, account number, ISIN, instrument code, position ID, loan number, swap confirmation number
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *uniqueness* — Each instrument has exactly one identifier within its source system, and that identifier is stable over time | count of duplicate identifier values for live instruments within a single source system | target: 0
  - *completeness* — Every risk record carries a populated instrument identifier | count of risk records with null instrument identifier | target: 0
  - *consistency* — The instrument identifier used in risk data matches the identifier used for the same instrument in the general ledger | count of instrument records in risk systems with no matching record in the GL by the same key | threshold: aligned to reconciliation tolerance

---

**CDE-05 — Risk Classification / Risk Type Code**

- **Definition:** The code or taxonomy value that classifies an exposure or position according to its primary risk type (e.g. credit risk, market risk, counterparty credit risk, liquidity risk) and, where applicable, sub-type (e.g. single-name vs. securitisation, trading book vs. banking book).
- **Why critical:** It determines which aggregation logic, capital model, and reporting template applies to each exposure. Misclassification shifts exposures between risk silos, causing both over- and under-statement in specific reports. It drives the risk calculation appearing in every Principle 7 report.
- **Risk types:** Cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 — A single misclassification (e.g. trading book vs. banking book) propagates into capital, VaR, and regulatory reports simultaneously.
- **Driven by:** Principle 7 (¶53(b)) — *"Automated and manual edit and reasonableness checks, including an inventory of the validation rules that are applied to quantitative information"*; Principle 4 (¶42) — *"each system should make clear the specific approach used to aggregate exposures for any given risk measure."*
- **Search terms:** risk type, risk category, book type, banking book flag, trading book flag, product type, asset class, sub-product code, classification code, risk bucket
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *validity* — Every exposure record carries a risk classification value drawn from the bank's approved taxonomy | count of records with classification values not present in the approved reference list | target: 0
  - *completeness* — No exposure record is unclassified | count of records with null or blank risk classification | target: 0
  - *consistency* — The risk classification assigned to an instrument is consistent across all systems that hold that instrument (e.g. risk system, regulatory reporting system, GL) | count of instruments where classification differs between risk system and regulatory reporting system | target: 0

---

**CDE-06 — Business Line / Organisational Unit Code**

- **Definition:** The code identifying the business line, division, or organisational unit responsible for or associated with a risk exposure — used as a primary aggregation dimension in risk reports.
- **Why critical:** Principle 4 explicitly requires risk data to be available by business line for aggregation. Without a reliable business line code, group-level reports cannot be decomposed and concentration by business line cannot be identified.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, liquidity, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 — Essential for completeness and drill-down reporting, but its failure typically distorts sub-totals rather than total exposure.
- **Driven by:** Principle 4 (¶41, header) — *"Data should be available by business line, legal entity, asset type, industry, region and other groupings, as relevant for the risk in question."*
- **Search terms:** business line code, division code, desk code, cost centre, profit centre, LOB code, segment code, product line, organisational unit
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *completeness* — Every exposure record carries a populated business line code | count of records with null business line code | target: 0
  - *validity* — Every business line code resolves to an active entry in the bank's organisational hierarchy | count of codes not present in the current organisational reference data | target: 0
  - *consistency* — The business line code on a risk record matches the business line attributed to the same instrument in management accounting systems | count of mismatches between risk and management accounting for matched instruments | threshold: to be set by the bank

---

**CDE-07 — Geography / Country of Risk Code**

- **Definition:** The code identifying the country or jurisdiction to which a risk exposure is attributed for aggregation purposes — typically the country of the obligor's domicile, the country of the asset's location, or the country of the issuer, depending on risk type.
- **Why critical:** Principle 4 requires aggregation by geography; Principle 6 (¶50) specifically cites country credit exposure aggregation as an example of required ad hoc capability. Geographic concentration is also a named component of comprehensiveness under Principle 8.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, concentration
- **Criticality:** 2 — Critical for concentration risk and stress scenarios (e.g. sovereign crisis); failure distorts geographic concentration reports but not total exposure.
- **Driven by:** Principle 6 (¶50) — *"a bank should be able to aggregate risk data quickly on country credit exposures as of a specified date based on a list of countries."*; Principle 4 header — *"Data should be available by… region."*
- **Search terms:** country code, country of risk, domicile country, booking country, obligor country, issuer country, risk country, geographic region, ISO country code
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *validity* — Every country code is a valid ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 or alpha-3 code, or an equivalent approved reference value | count of records with unrecognised country codes | target: 0
  - *completeness* — Every credit and market exposure carries a populated country of risk code | count of in-scope records with null country code | target: 0
  - *consistency* — The country of risk assigned to a counterparty is consistent between the counterparty master and individual exposure records referencing that counterparty | count of exposures where country of risk differs from the country held on the counterparty master without an approved override | target: 0

---

**CDE-08 — Industry / Sector Classification Code**

- **Definition:** The code classifying a counterparty or issuer according to an industry or economic sector taxonomy (e.g. NACE, SIC, GICS, or the bank's internal scheme) — used to aggregate and monitor sectoral concentration.
- **Why critical:** Sectoral concentration is explicitly named in Principle 8 (¶57) as a required component of credit risk reporting. It is also cited in Principle 5 (¶46(c)) as a critical data item for timely reporting. Without it, industry-level concentrations cannot be identified.
- **Risk types:** Credit, concentration
- **Criticality:** 2 — Necessary for concentration monitoring; failure leads to inability to identify emerging sectoral stress but does not destroy total exposure figures.
- **Driven by:** Principle 8 (¶57) — *"Risk management reports should include exposure and position information for all significant risk areas… single name, country and industry sector for credit risk."*; Principle 5 (¶46(c)) — *"market concentrations by sector and region data."*
- **Search terms:** industry code, sector code, NACE code, SIC code, GICS sector, industry classification, counterparty sector, borrower industry
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *completeness* — Every corporate and financial institution counterparty carries a populated industry classification | count of counterparties above a materiality threshold with null sector code | target: 0
  - *validity* — Every sector code is drawn from the bank's approved sector taxonomy | count of sector codes not present in the approved reference list | target: 0
  - *consistency* — Sector classification is consistent for the same counterparty across credit and market risk systems | count of counterparties where sector code differs between credit and market risk systems | target: 0

---

**CDE-09 — Reporting Date / As-Of Date**

- **Definition:** The date as of which an exposure, position, or risk measure is stated — the temporal anchor for all risk data aggregation and the reference point for reconciliation and report production.
- **Why critical:** Timeliness compliance depends on every record carrying a correct and consistent as-of date. Mismatched or missing as-of dates cause records from different time periods to be mixed in an aggregate, destroying both accuracy and completeness of the snapshot.
- **Risk types:** Cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 — Every risk metric is time-stamped; a wrong or missing as-of date invalidates the data point entirely for reporting purposes.
- **Driven by:** Principle 5 (¶44) — *"risk data aggregation capabilities should ensure that it is able to produce aggregate risk information on a timely basis"*; Principle 7 (¶53(a)) — *"Defined requirements and processes to reconcile reports to risk data."*
- **Search terms:** as-of date, reference date, position date, reporting date, valuation date, effective date, snapshot date, trade date, settlement date
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *completeness* — Every risk record carries a populated as-of date | count of records with null as-of date | target: 0
  - *validity* — As-of dates fall within expected reporting calendar bounds (not future-dated beyond T+0, not more than the maximum permitted lag in the past) | count of records with as-of dates outside valid bounds for the reporting cycle | target: 0
  - *timeliness* — The as-of date on records available for a given report cycle matches the required reference date for that cycle | count of records in a reporting population whose as-of date does not match the report's reference date | target: 0
  - *consistency* — The as-of date used in risk data matches the corresponding date in the GL for the same reconciliation population | count of mismatches in as-of date between risk and GL for matched instruments | target: 0

---

**CDE-10 — Collateral Value and Type**

- **Definition:** The assessed monetary value of collateral held against a credit or counterparty exposure, together with the code identifying the type of collateral (e.g. cash, real estate, financial security, guarantee). Used to compute net exposure and Loss Given Default.
- **Why critical:** Net credit exposure calculations depend on collateral values. Stale or misclassified collateral directly inflates or deflates reported net exposure and capital requirements. Off-balance-sheet collateral coverage must be captured to meet the completeness requirement of ¶41.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty
- **Criticality:** 2 — Materially affects net exposure and capital; however, gross exposure reporting is possible without it, making this less foundational than the counterparty or instrument identifiers.
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (¶36(a)) — *"Controls surrounding risk data should be as robust as those applicable to accounting data"*; Principle 4 (¶41) — off-balance-sheet coverage requirement applies to collateral arrangements.
- **Search terms:** collateral value, collateral type, collateral code, haircut, LTV, loan to value, security value, eligible collateral, collateral amount, pledge value
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *timeliness* — Collateral values are revalued at the frequency prescribed by risk policy (daily for market-price-sensitive collateral, periodic for real estate) | count of collateral records whose last valuation date exceeds the permitted staleness threshold | threshold: policy-defined by collateral type
  - *validity* — Collateral type codes are drawn from the approved collateral classification taxonomy | count of records with unrecognised collateral type codes | target: 0
  - *completeness* — Every secured exposure record references at least one collateral record, or carries an explicit null-collateral flag | count of secured exposures with neither a linked collateral record nor a null-collateral indicator | target: 0
  - *accuracy* — Collateral values in the risk system reconcile to values held in the collateral management system of record | count of collateral records where the value in the risk system differs from the collateral system value beyond an agreed tolerance | threshold: aligned to the bank's credit risk materiality standard

---

**CDE-11 — Liquidity Risk Indicator — Cash Flow / Settlement Amount**

- **Definition:** The expected cash inflow or outflow associated with a financial instrument or contractual obligation on a specified settlement or maturity date — the primary input to liquidity gap and cash flow ladder reports.
- **Why critical:** Principle 5 (¶46(d)) names cash flows and funding as critical risk data requiring rapid production under stress. Liquidity crises are precisely the scenario where data latency is most dangerous.
- **Risk types:** Liquidity
- **Criticality:** 3 — In a stress scenario, intraday or overnight liquidity position reporting depends entirely on the accuracy and timeliness of cash flow data.
- **Driven by:** Principle 5 (¶46(d)) — *"Liquidity risk indicators such as cash flows/settlements and funding"*; Principle 5 (¶45) — *"capable of producing aggregated risk data rapidly during times of stress/crisis for all critical risks."*
- **Search terms:** cash flow, settlement amount, maturity cash flow, contractual cash flow, expected settlement, funding need, payment obligation, liquidity outflow, LCR cash flow, NSFR cash flow
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *timeliness* — Cash flow records are available as of the required intraday or end-of-day cut-off for liquidity reporting | count of cash flow records not available within the agreed production window | target: 0 for critical liquidity reports
  - *completeness* — All contractual cash flows for in-scope instruments are captured, including contingent outflows (e.g. undrawn committed lines) | count of in-scope instruments with no associated cash flow projection record | target: 0
  - *accuracy* — Projected cash flow amounts reconcile to scheduled payment amounts in the bank's payments and settlement systems | sum of absolute differences between risk system cash flows and payment system scheduled amounts for the same instruments | threshold: policy-defined liquidity materiality

---

**CDE-12 — Market Risk Position / Mark-to-Market Value**

- **Definition:** The current marked-to-market value of a trading position or instrument — the monetary amount at which the position is carried in the trading book, used as the basis for VaR, PnL, and market risk capital calculations.
- **Why critical:** Principle 5 (¶46(c)) names trading exposures and positions as critical data requiring rapid production. The MTM value is the direct input to market risk aggregation and limit monitoring.
- **Risk types:** Market, counterparty
- **Criticality:** 2 — Essential for market risk reporting; however, market risk is typically served by more mature automated systems than credit risk, reducing the relative likelihood of data catalog-addressable failures.
- **Driven by:** Principle 5 (¶46(c)) — *"Trading exposures, positions, operating limits, and market concentrations by sector and region data."*; Principle 7 (¶52) — accuracy requirement for risk management reports.
- **Search terms:** MTM value, mark-to-market, fair value, position value, PV, NPV, current market value, dirty price, clean price, position PnL
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *timeliness* — MTM values are produced as of the end-of-day or intraday cut-off required for the relevant risk report | count of positions with MTM values not refreshed within the required production window | target: 0
  - *accuracy* — MTM values in risk aggregation systems reconcile to values held in the front-office pricing system of record | count of positions where the risk system MTM differs from the front-office system value beyond an agreed tolerance | threshold: VaR materiality standard
  - *completeness* — Every open trading position carries a current MTM value | count of open positions with null MTM value | target: 0

---

**CDE-13 — Risk Limit and Limit Utilisation**

- **Definition:** The approved limit for a risk metric (e.g. counterparty credit limit, VaR limit, large exposure limit) and the current utilisation of that limit expressed as an amount or percentage — together forming the basis of limit breach identification and reporting.
- **Why critical:** Principle 8 (¶58) requires reports to provide information in the context of limits and risk appetite. Limit data must be traceable to the governance decision that approved it, and utilisation must be computed from the same exposure data as the broader risk reports. A missing or stale limit makes breach detection impossible.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, liquidity, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 — Required for Principle 8 comprehensiveness; a limit not captured in the catalog does not distort the exposure figure, but it makes the report incomplete.
- **Driven by:** Principle 8 (¶58) — *"Reports should identify emerging risk concentrations, provide information in the context of limits and risk appetite/tolerance."*
- **Search terms:** risk limit, credit limit, exposure limit, VaR limit, limit amount, limit utilisation, limit headroom, approved limit, concentration limit, large exposure limit
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *validity* — Every limit record references an approved governance decision (e.g. credit committee minute, board resolution) | count of limit records with no traceable approval reference | target: 0
  - *timeliness* — Limit records are updated to reflect approved changes within the governance cycle | count of limit records where the effective date of a known approval change has passed but the limit has not been updated | target: 0
  - *completeness* — Every material risk dimension for which a limit exists has a corresponding limit record in the risk system | count of governance-approved limits with no matching record in the risk limit system | target: 0

## 3. Cross-Cutting Data Quality Requirements

These requirements span multiple CDEs and cannot be assigned to a single element. They represent structural integrity checks across the data estate.

---

### XCR-01 — Risk-to-Finance Reconciliation

**What it covers:** The total gross exposure in risk systems must reconcile to the corresponding on-balance-sheet balances in the general ledger. This is the primary accuracy control required by ¶36(c).

**Regulatory basis:** Principle 3 (¶36(c)) — *"Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate, to ensure that the risk data is accurate."*

**Rule intent:** The sum of on-balance-sheet exposure amounts in the risk aggregation layer equals the corresponding GL balance for the same population of instruments, within an agreed materiality threshold.

**Measurement:** Absolute and percentage difference between the sum of exposure amounts in risk systems and the corresponding GL balances, computed at agreed reconciliation break levels (entity, product type, currency).

**Suggested threshold:** Analogous to accounting materiality as required by ¶56 — the bank must define and document this threshold explicitly; it cannot be an arbitrary percentage.

**CDEs spanned:** CDE-01 (Counterparty Identifier), CDE-03 (Exposure Amount), CDE-04 (Instrument Identifier), CDE-05 (Risk Classification), CDE-09 (Reporting Date)

---

### XCR-02 — Cross-System Identifier Consistency

**What it covers:** The same counterparty, entity, and instrument must carry the same identifier across every system in which it appears — credit, market, liquidity, operations, and GL. This is the precondition for group-wide aggregation.

**Regulatory basis:** Principle 2 (¶33) — *"use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities, counterparties, customers and accounts."*

**Rule intent:** For every entity appearing in more than one risk or finance system, the identifier used in each system resolves to the same golden-source record without manual mapping.

**Measurement:** Count of cross-system entity pairs (same real-world party appearing in two systems) where the identifiers do not match and no approved mapping exists.

**Suggested threshold:** Target of zero unresolved identifier conflicts for counterparties above a materiality threshold; residual conflicts below the threshold must be inventoried and explained.

**CDEs spanned:** CDE-01 (Counterparty Identifier), CDE-02 (Legal Entity Identifier), CDE-04 (Instrument Identifier)

---

### XCR-03 — Completeness of Group Coverage (Population Completeness)

**What it covers:** The risk data population must include all in-scope legal entities, business lines, and off-balance-sheet items. Gaps in coverage are not merely missing values in existing records — they are entire populations absent from the aggregation.

**Regulatory basis:** Principle 4 (¶41) — *"A bank's risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures, including those that are off-balance sheet"*; Principle 4 (¶43) — *"Supervisors expect banks to produce aggregated risk data that is complete and to measure and monitor the completeness of their risk data."*

**Rule intent:** Every legal entity in the bank's authoritative group structure, and every product type in the bank's product taxonomy, has a corresponding presence in the risk data population. Absence is either a documented approved exclusion or a deficiency requiring remediation.

**Measurement:** Count of legal entities in the group structure master with zero exposure records in the risk aggregation layer; count of product types in the product taxonomy with zero records in the risk data population.

**Suggested threshold:** Zero undocumented absences. Any deliberate exclusion must be recorded with a materiality justification and approved by senior management (¶30).

**CDEs spanned:** CDE-01 (Counterparty Identifier), CDE-02 (Legal Entity Identifier), CDE-05 (Risk Classification), CDE-06 (Business Line Code)

---

### XCR-04 — Timeliness of Critical Risk Data Under Stress

**What it covers:** For the risk types named in ¶46 as critical — large corporate credit, counterparty credit, trading positions, liquidity, and operational indicators — data must be available within the production window required for stress reporting, which may be intraday.

**Regulatory basis:** Principle 5 (¶45) — *"Banks need to build their risk systems to be capable of producing aggregated risk data rapidly during times of stress/crisis for all critical risks."*; Principle 10 (¶71) — *"all relevant and critical credit, market and liquidity position/exposure reports are available within a very short period of time."*

**Rule intent:** For each critical risk data set, the lag between the as-of date and the point at which the data is available for aggregation does not exceed the production window defined for that risk type under both normal and stress conditions.

**Measurement:** For each critical risk type, maximum and mean lag in hours between the as-of date/time of records and the time of availability in the aggregation layer, measured over a rolling period and separately under simulated stress conditions.

**Suggested threshold:** Defined per risk type by the bank; for intraday liquidity and trading positions, the threshold may be minutes; for credit, end-of-day T+0 or T+1 depending on the bank's stress reporting requirements.

**CDEs spanned:** CDE-09 (Reporting Date), CDE-11 (Cash Flow/Settlement Amount), CDE-12 (MTM Value), CDE-03 (Exposure Amount)

## 4. Out of Scope — What a Data Catalog Cannot Deliver

A data catalog, however well-governed, is a metadata and data quality management instrument. BCBS 239 contains a substantial set of requirements that are organisational, process, and reporting obligations. Treating catalog compliance as BCBS 239 compliance would be a material misrepresentation. The following are out of scope:

---

### Principle 8 — Comprehensiveness of Risk Reports (¶57–60)

Principle 8 requires that risk reports cover all material risk areas, include concentration analysis, forward-looking forecasts, stress test results, and capital adequacy information. It also requires that reports be scaled to the size and complexity of the bank and that they propose recommendations for action.

**Why a catalog cannot satisfy this:** A catalog documents what data elements exist and their quality. It cannot generate, validate, or govern the analytical content of a risk report — the selection of risk metrics, the appropriateness of the forward-looking scenarios, or whether the report's coverage is commensurate with the bank's risk profile. This requires report design standards, risk governance frameworks, and senior management review. What is needed instead: a report inventory governed by the risk function, a documented mapping between reports and material risk types, and board/senior management review of report scope.

---

### Principle 9 — Clarity and Usefulness of Risk Reports (¶61–69)

Principle 9 requires that reports be clear, concise, tailored to their audience, appropriately balanced between quantitative and qualitative content, and that recipients periodically confirm the reports meet their needs (¶69). It also requires that the bank develop an inventory and classification of risk data items with a reference to concepts used in reports (¶67).

**Note on ¶67:** The requirement to develop an inventory and classification of risk data items with conceptual definitions is the closest BCBS 239 comes to mandating a data catalog or business glossary. A catalog can partially address this by housing the business glossary and linking data elements to reports. However, the broader Principle 9 obligation — ensuring reports are understandable and useful to their recipients — is a communications and governance obligation that catalog metadata cannot satisfy. What is needed instead: report template standards, a governed business glossary (which can live in a catalog), recipient feedback processes, and reporting governance committees.

---

### Principle 10 — Frequency of Risk Reporting (¶70–71)

Principle 10 requires that the board and senior management set report frequency, that frequency is increased during stress, and that the bank routinely tests its ability to produce accurate reports within established timeframes. It explicitly addresses intraday reporting capability for critical risks.

**Why a catalog cannot satisfy this:** Frequency requirements are operational SLAs owned by the risk reporting function and IT. A catalog can record the intended refresh frequency of a data element as metadata, and DQ monitoring can measure whether data arrives on time. But the *setting* of frequency requirements, the governance of SLA changes, the testing of stress-scenario production capacity, and the board approval of those frequencies are management and operational obligations. What is needed instead: a report production SLA register governed by risk operations and IT, regular production testing (including fire drills), and board/senior management sign-off on frequency standards.

---

### Principle 11 — Distribution and Confidentiality (¶72–74)

Principle 11 requires that procedures exist for rapid collection and dissemination of risk reports to appropriate recipients, while maintaining confidentiality. It requires periodic confirmation that relevant recipients are receiving timely reports (¶73).

**Why a catalog cannot satisfy this:** Report distribution is a function of the bank's report delivery infrastructure, access control frameworks, and governance processes. A catalog can record data stewards and business owners as metadata, and it can implement data access policies and privacy classifications at the data element level. However, the distribution of *reports* (as opposed to raw data) to named individuals, the controls on who receives what report, and the confirmation that recipients are appropriate are obligations of the risk reporting and IT governance functions. What is needed instead: a report distribution register, role-based access controls on reporting platforms, and periodic recipient review by senior management.

---

### Principle 1 — Board and Senior Management Accountability (¶27–31)

Principle 1's requirements for board review and approval of the RDAP framework (¶28), independent validation of aggregation and reporting processes (¶29(a)), and senior management awareness of aggregation limitations (¶30) are governance obligations at the organisational level.

**Why a catalog partially addresses this:** A catalog can provide the documented inventory of data elements, data owners, and DQ monitoring results that an independent validation team would review. It can also surface known data quality limitations. However, the independent validation process itself, the board review and approval of the framework, and the senior management acknowledgement of limitations are governance acts that require documented decisions, committee minutes, and audit trails outside any catalog. What is needed instead: a Data Governance framework with documented roles and accountabilities, an independent validation programme (model risk or internal audit), and board-level reporting on RDAP compliance status.

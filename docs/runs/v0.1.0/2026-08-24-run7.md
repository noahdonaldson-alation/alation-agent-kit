## 1. Objectives and Scope

### What the regulation is trying to achieve

- **End fragmented, manual risk data aggregation.** Banks must be able to aggregate all material risk exposures â including off-balance-sheet positions â across legal entities, business lines, asset types, industries and geographies, largely through automated means that minimise the probability of errors. (Â¶36: *"Controls surrounding risk data should be as robust as those applicable to accounting data."*; Â¶41: *"A bank's risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures, including those that are off-balance sheet."*)

- **Establish a single, authoritative, well-defined data foundation.** Banks are expected to maintain a data dictionary so that concepts are defined consistently across the organisation, to strive toward a single authoritative source per risk type, and to use unified naming conventions and identifiers across legal entities, counterparties, customers and accounts. (Â¶37: *"A bank should have a 'dictionary' of the concepts used, such that data is defined consistently across an organisation."*; Â¶33: *"â¦use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities, counterparties, customers and accounts."*)

- **Ensure risk reports are accurate, reconciled and timely â including under stress.** Risk data must be reconciled with source systems and accounting data. Accuracy requirements are explicitly compared to accounting materiality. In stress or crisis, certain aggregated positions must be available intraday. (Â¶36(c): *"Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate."*; Â¶45: *"Banks need to build their risk systems to be capable of producing aggregated risk data rapidly during times of stress/crisis."*)

- **Assign and enforce data ownership with documented controls.** Roles and responsibilities for data quality and ownership must be established for both business and IT functions, covering the full data lifecycle. (Â¶34: *"Roles and responsibilities should be established as they relate to the ownership and quality of risk data and information for both the business and IT functions."*)

- **Embed risk data governance into board-level accountability.** Senior management must understand the limitations of data aggregation coverage. The board must be aware of those limitations in the reports it receives and is responsible for determining its own risk reporting requirements. (Â¶30: *"Senior management should be fully aware of and understand the limitations that prevent full risk data aggregation."*; Â¶31: *"A bank's board is responsible for determining its own risk reporting requirements."*)

- **Enable adaptable, on-demand aggregation.** The data architecture must support ad hoc queries, drill-down, scenario analysis and stress testing â not just scheduled reports â including the ability to slice exposures by country, industry, or any other dimension on request. (Â¶50: *"Supervisors expect banks to be able to generate subsets of data based on requested scenarios or resulting from economic events."*)

### Who it applies to

Principles 1â11 are addressed directly to **global systemically important banks (G-SIBs)** as the primary compliance population (as defined in the Basel Committee's scope at the time of publication), with an expectation that national supervisors would extend them to domestic systemically important banks (D-SIBs). The principles apply at the **banking group** level â not just the parent entity â meaning subsidiaries, branches and material legal entities within the consolidated group are in scope.

**CDE-01 â Legal Entity Identifier**
- **Definition:** The unique, authoritative identifier assigned to each legal entity within the banking group, used consistently across all risk, finance and operational systems.
- **Why critical:** Without a stable legal entity identifier, risk exposures cannot be aggregated across the group, subsidiaries cannot be included or excluded cleanly, and regulatory capital cannot be computed at the correct consolidation perimeter. It is the primary joining key for all group-wide aggregation.
- **Risk types:** Cross-cutting (credit, market, liquidity, operational, concentration)
- **Criticality:** 3 â Every other CDE in this list depends on the legal entity identifier to locate exposure within the group structure. Its absence or inconsistency directly prevents group-level aggregation.
- **Driven by:** Principle 2 (Â¶33) â *"â¦use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities, counterparties, customers and accounts."*; Principle 4 (Â¶41) â *"A bank's risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures."*
- **Search terms:** Legal entity ID, LEI, entity code, group entity code, organisation identifier, subsidiary code, consolidation entity
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *uniqueness* â Each legal entity within the group has exactly one authoritative identifier, with no duplicates across systems | count of entity records sharing the same identifier value; count of distinct identifier values mapped to the same entity name | zero duplicates
  - *completeness* â Every risk exposure record carries a non-null legal entity identifier | proportion of exposure records with null or unmapped legal entity identifier | <0.1%
  - *consistency* â The legal entity identifier resolves to the same entity definition in the risk system, the general ledger and the group consolidation system | count of entity identifiers present in the risk system but absent or differently mapped in the GL | zero unreconciled differences
  - *validity* â Each legal entity identifier resolves to an active, recognised entity in the authoritative group entity register | count of identifiers that do not match any record in the authoritative register | zero unmatched

**CDE-02 â Counterparty Identifier**
- **Definition:** The unique identifier assigned to each counterparty (borrower, derivative counterparty, issuer, or guarantor) that allows all exposures to that counterparty to be aggregated across products, books and legal entities.
- **Why critical:** Concentration risk and large exposure limits are computed by aggregating all exposures to a single counterparty. Without a reliable, cross-system counterparty identifier, a bank cannot produce an aggregated credit exposure to a large corporate borrower â the first example of a critical risk named in the text.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, concentration
- **Criticality:** 3 â Directly named as a critical aggregation requirement; also the basis for counterparty credit risk exposure calculation under Principle 5 (Â¶46(a),(b)).
- **Driven by:** Principle 5 (Â¶46(a),(b)) â *"The aggregated credit exposure to a large corporate borrowerâ¦ Counterparty credit risk exposures, including, for example, derivatives."*; Principle 2 (Â¶33) â *"â¦single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data includingâ¦ counterparties."*
- **Search terms:** Counterparty ID, obligor ID, customer ID, borrower ID, BCBS counterparty reference, legal entity counterparty, CCP identifier, issuer ID
- **Data quality requirements:**

**CDE-03 â Exposure Amount (Gross and Net)**
- **Definition:** The monetary amount of a risk exposure to a counterparty or instrument, expressed both before and after the application of credit risk mitigants (netting, collateral), in a defined reporting currency.
- **Why critical:** It is the primary quantity that is aggregated to produce group-level credit, counterparty and concentration risk figures. Inaccuracy in this element directly distorts the risk reports the board and senior management rely upon. Reconciliation of this figure to the general ledger is a primary accuracy control.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, concentration
- **Criticality:** 3 â The numerics that all risk reports are built from; their accuracy is the subject of explicit reconciliation requirements in Principle 3 and Principle 7.
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (Â¶36(c)) â *"Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate, to ensure that the risk data is accurate."*; Principle 7 (Â¶53(a)) â *"Defined requirements and processes to reconcile reports to risk data."*
- **Search terms:** Exposure amount, notional amount, outstanding balance, drawn amount, EAD, exposure at default, net exposure, gross exposure, mark-to-market value, fair value
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *accuracy* â The sum of exposure amounts in the risk system reconciles to the corresponding carrying value or notional in the general ledger within an agreed materiality tolerance | absolute and percentage difference between risk system aggregate and GL aggregate by portfolio segment | within accounting materiality threshold (consistent with Â¶56)
  - *completeness* â No material exposure is missing; off-balance-sheet items are included | count and value of off-balance-sheet commitments present in the credit administration system but absent from the risk aggregation dataset | zero unreconciled off-balance-sheet items
  - *timeliness* â Exposure amounts reflect the position as of the required reporting date; in stress scenarios, intraday positions are available for critical books | maximum lag between trade execution or GL posting and reflection of updated exposure amount in the risk aggregation layer | within SLA defined per risk type (intraday for trading/liquidity; T+1 for credit)
  - *validity* â Exposure amounts are expressed in a stated currency; negative values exist only for valid netting or short positions and are documented | count of exposure records with null currency code or with negative gross exposure lacking a documented basis | zero undocumented negatives

**CDE-04 â Risk Classification / Risk Type Code**
- **Definition:** The classification that assigns each exposure or position to a risk category (credit risk, market risk, liquidity risk, operational risk, counterparty credit risk) and, where relevant, to a sub-category (e.g. drawn/undrawn, trading/banking book).
- **Why critical:** Aggregation is performed within and across risk types. If an exposure is misclassified, it is counted in the wrong risk bucket â or not counted at all. Principle 4 requires aggregation to work regardless of the risk aggregation system chosen, which demands consistent classification as the common language.
- **Risk types:** Cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 â A misclassification does not destroy data; it misroutes it. However, systematic misclassification of even a small population can materially distort a risk figure.
- **Driven by:** Principle 4 (Â¶42) â *"Each system should make clear the specific approach used to aggregate exposures for any given risk measure, in order to allow the board and senior management to assess the results properly."*; Principle 8 (Â¶57) â *"Risk management reports should include exposure and position information for all significant risk areas."*
- **Search terms:** Risk type, risk category, risk classification, book type, trading book flag, banking book flag, product type, instrument class
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *validity* â Every exposure record carries a risk classification that belongs to the bank's approved taxonomy | count of records with a risk type code not present in the approved classification scheme | zero invalid codes
  - *completeness* â No exposure record has a null risk classification | proportion of exposure records with null or blank risk type | <0.1%
  - *consistency* â The risk classification assigned in the originating system (e.g. trade booking system) matches the classification used in the risk aggregation layer and in the regulatory capital system | count of records where classification differs across these three systems | zero unreconciled discrepancies

**CDE-05 â Business Line / Organisational Unit**
- **Definition:** The identifier or code that assigns each exposure or transaction to a business line (e.g. retail banking, corporate banking, trading, wealth management) and, where relevant, to a desk or sub-unit.
- **Why critical:** Principle 4 explicitly requires data to be available by business line. Without this dimension, a bank cannot slice risk by organisational unit, cannot attribute concentration to a business, and cannot identify whether a subsidiary is included or excluded from aggregation.
- **Risk types:** Cross-cutting (credit, market, liquidity, concentration)
- **Criticality:** 2 â Required for slicing and attribution; its absence prevents compliance with the completeness principle's dimensional requirements.

**CDE-06 â Geography / Country of Risk**
- **Definition:** The country or jurisdiction assigned to an exposure as the primary location of risk â typically the country of the counterparty's domicile or the country to which the credit risk is considered to transfer in a stress scenario (country of ultimate risk).

- **Risk types:** Credit, concentration, market

**CDE-08 â Instrument / Product Type**
- **Definition:** The classification of a financial instrument or product into a standardised type (e.g. loan, revolving credit facility, bond, OTC derivative, repo, exchange-traded derivative, contingent commitment).
- **Why critical:** Aggregation by asset type is required under Principle 4. - **Risk types:** Credit, market, counterparty, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 â Drives risk calculation routing; errors produce wrong risk figures without necessarily producing null values (hard to detect by completeness checks alone).

**CDE-09 â Maturity / Tenor**
- **Definition:** The contractual maturity date or residual tenor of an instrument â the date on which principal is due to be repaid or the contract expires.
- **Why critical:** Maturity bucketing is fundamental to liquidity risk aggregation (cash flow ladders, funding gap analysis) and is also used in credit risk (maturity adjustment in capital calculations) and market risk (duration, sensitivity). - **Risk types:** Liquidity, credit, market
- **Criticality:** 2 â Directly affects liquidity risk aggregation, which is time-sensitive under stress conditions (Â¶46(d)).
- **Driven by:** Principle 5 (Â¶46(d)) â *"Liquidity risk indicators such as cash flows/settlements and funding."*; Principle 4 (Â¶41) â covers all material risk exposures including off-balance-sheet.
- **Search terms:** Maturity date, final maturity, legal maturity, contractual maturity, residual tenor, settlement date, cash flow date, expiry date
- **Data quality requirements:**

  - *accuracy* â The maturity date in the risk system matches the maturity date in the loan administration or trade confirmation system of record | count of instruments where maturity date differs between the risk system and the system of record | zero unreconciled differences

**CDE-10 â Collateral / Credit Risk Mitigation Reference**
- **Definition:** The identifier and characteristics (type, value, haircut, eligibility) of collateral or other credit risk mitigants (guarantees, credit derivatives) linked to an exposure, used to compute net exposure.
- **Why critical:** Net exposure â the figure that actually drives regulatory capital and risk reports â cannot be computed without accurate collateral linkage. Principle 3 requires accuracy equivalent to accounting data.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty
- **Criticality:** 2 â Affects the net exposure figure, which feeds capital calculations and risk reports, but the failure mode is financial overstatement or understatement rather than complete data absence.
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (Â¶36(a)) â *"Controls surrounding risk data should be as robust as those applicable to accounting data."*; Principle 7 (Â¶56) â *"If omission or misstatement could influence the risk decisions of users, this may be considered material."*

- **Data quality requirements:**

**CDE-11 â Limit / Risk Appetite Threshold**
- **Definition:** The approved numeric limit assigned to a counterparty, portfolio, sector or legal entity â such as a single-name credit limit, a trading book VaR limit, or a liquidity coverage threshold â against which current exposure is measured.

- **Risk types:** Credit, market, liquidity, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 â Does not affect exposure accuracy directly but is required for the exposure figures to be interpreted in context, as explicitly required by Principle 8.

## 3. Cross-Cutting Data Quality Requirements

### XDQ-01 â Risk-to-Finance (GL) Reconciliation

**Spans:** CDE-03 (Exposure Amount), CDE-05 (Business Line), CDE-01 (Legal Entity Identifier), CDE-12 (Data Source Provenance)

**Requirement intent:** Risk data must reconcile to the general ledger at appropriate levels of aggregation. Any unexplained difference â by legal entity, business line, or portfolio â must be escalated and resolved. This is the primary accuracy control required by Principle 3 and Principle 7.

- *consistency* â The sum of exposure amounts in the risk aggregation layer reconciles to the sum of carrying values (or notionals, as appropriate) in the general ledger, segmented by legal entity and business line | monetary value and percentage of unexplained reconciling items by legal entity and business line | within accounting materiality as defined under Â¶56
- *completeness* â All portfolios and legal entities that are in scope for the risk consolidation perimeter are also present in the reconciliation | count of legal entities present in the GL but absent from the risk reconciliation run | zero
- *timeliness* â The reconciliation is completed within the defined close cycle; open items are aged and escalated | count and value of reconciling items open beyond the defined escalation threshold | tracked with documented escalation path

### XDQ-02 â Group Perimeter Completeness Check

**Spans:** CDE-01 (Legal Entity Identifier), CDE-05 (Business Line), CDE-04 (Risk Classification), CDE-12 (Data Source Provenance)

**Requirement intent:** The group risk aggregation must cover all material legal entities. A legal entity that is in the banking group but absent from the risk data is an aggregation failure. - *consistency* â The group perimeter used in risk aggregation matches the consolidation perimeter used in financial reporting | count of entities in the financial consolidation perimeter that are absent from the risk perimeter without a documented regulatory basis for exclusion | zero undocumented exclusions

### XDQ-03 â Cross-System Counterparty Identity Resolution

**Spans:** CDE-02 (Counterparty Identifier), CDE-07 (Industry Classification), CDE-06 (Geography), CDE-10 (Collateral)

**Requirement intent:** The same counterparty must be recognised as the same entity across all systems that book exposures (loan management, trading, repo, derivatives). - *consistency* â Attributes that are assigned at the counterparty master level (sector code, country of risk, internal credit rating) are used consistently by all downstream booking systems, not locally overridden | count of records where a booking system carries a sector or country code that differs from the counterparty master without a documented product-level override rule | zero undocumented overrides

### XDQ-04 â Timeliness SLA Monitoring by Risk Type

**Spans:** CDE-03 (Exposure Amount), CDE-09 (Maturity/Tenor), CDE-11 (Limit), CDE-12 (Data Source Provenance)

**Requirement intent:** Different risk types have different timeliness requirements. ## 4. Out of Scope â What a Catalog Cannot Deliver

The following requirements arise from Principles 1 and 7â11. They are real regulatory obligations. Cataloguing CDEs and monitoring data quality are necessary conditions for meeting them, but they are not sufficient, and no extension of catalog functionality closes these gaps.

---

### 4.1 Board and Senior Management Governance (Principle 1, Â¶27â31)

The regulation requires the board to approve the risk data aggregation framework, to be aware of its limitations, and to ensure adequate resources are deployed. A data catalog can make the limitations visible â by surfacing coverage gaps, data quality failures, and unmapped entities â but it cannot constitute the governance framework, substitute for board review, or ensure that resources are allocated. What is needed instead: a formal data governance programme with a board-approved policy, a data governance committee with executive sponsorship, and a documented process by which data quality findings from the catalog are escalated to senior management and, where material, to the board.

---

### 4.2 Independent Validation (Principle 1, Â¶29(a))

The text requires that aggregation capabilities and reporting practices be *independently validated* by staff with IT, data and reporting expertise. A catalog's data quality monitors are controls, not independent validation. They are operated by the data management function, not by an independent reviewer. What is needed instead: a second-line or internal audit review that examines the catalog's own coverage (are all CDEs catalogued?), the adequacy of the DQ rules (do they actually detect the failures they claim to?), and the completeness of lineage documentation â independent of the teams that built and run the catalog.

---

### 4.3 Report Comprehensiveness (Principle 8, Â¶57â60)

The regulation requires reports to cover all material risk areas and components, include forward-looking stress test results, and identify emerging concentrations in the context of risk appetite. A data catalog governs what data exists and how good it is. It has no view of whether a report omits a material risk area, whether stress test scenarios are appropriately severe, or whether the board is receiving the right depth and balance of information. What is needed instead: a reporting governance framework that maps required report content to approved risk appetite statements, that reviews report completeness against the taxonomy of material risks, and that tracks whether board-level reports include forward-looking content.

---

### 4.4 Clarity and Usefulness of Reports (Principle 9, Â¶61â69)

The regulation requires reports to be clear, appropriately balanced between quantitative and qualitative content, and tailored to the information needs of recipients at different levels of the organisation. A catalog governs source data, not how it is presented or interpreted. It cannot assess whether a board pack is comprehensible, whether the level of aggregation is appropriate, or whether the qualitative commentary adds genuine insight. What is needed instead: a report governance process that includes periodic recipient feedback (explicitly required by Â¶69), editorial standards for risk reports, and a classification of reports by audience tier with defined content standards per tier.

---

### 4.5 Reporting Frequency and Stress Readiness (Principle 10, Â¶70â71)

The regulation requires the bank to *routinely test* its ability to produce accurate reports within defined timeframes, particularly in stress. A catalog can monitor whether data is arriving within SLA in normal production â that is covered in XDQ-04 above. It cannot conduct or evidence a stress test of the reporting infrastructure, simulate a crisis scenario to verify intraday report production, or certify that the bank could produce all critical reports within a very short period during a real stress event. What is needed instead: a formal business continuity and stress-readiness testing programme for risk infrastructure, with documented test results, gap remediation tracking, and sign-off by senior management.

---

### 4.6 Report Distribution and Confidentiality (Principle 11, Â¶72â73)

The regulation requires procedures for rapid dissemination of reports to appropriate recipients while maintaining confidentiality, and periodic confirmation that relevant recipients receive timely reports. A data catalog manages access to data assets, and access control metadata is relevant to confidentiality at the data layer. But the regulation is concerned with report distribution â whether the right people receive the right report at the right time â which is a function of the reporting and distribution platform, access management, and a distribution confirmation process, not of catalog governance. What is needed instead: a report distribution register maintained in or alongside the reporting platform, with access controls, distribution confirmation, and periodic recipient reconciliation.

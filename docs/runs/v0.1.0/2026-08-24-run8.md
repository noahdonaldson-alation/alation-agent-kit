# BCBS 239 — Data Catalog Interpretation: What to Build and Govern

---

## 1. Objectives and Scope

### What the regulation is trying to achieve

- **Reliable risk aggregation at group level.** Banks must capture *all* material risk exposures across the entire banking group — by business line, legal entity, asset type, industry, and geography — so that no material exposure is invisible to aggregation (¶41: *"A bank's risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures, including those that are off-balance sheet."*)

- **Accuracy and integrity of risk data, equivalent to accounting standards.** Risk data controls must be as robust as those applied to financial accounting, with reconciliation back to authoritative sources and a drive toward a single authoritative source per risk type (¶36(a),(c),(d): *"Controls surrounding risk data should be as robust as those applicable to accounting data… Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate."*)

- **A common data language across the group.** Banks must maintain a dictionary of consistently defined concepts and integrated data taxonomies, including unified naming conventions for legal entities, counterparties, customers, and accounts (¶33: *"A bank should establish integrated data taxonomies and architecture across the banking group, which includes information on the characteristics of the data (metadata), as well as use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions."*; ¶37: *"A bank should have a 'dictionary' of the concepts used, such that data is defined consistently across an organisation."*)

- **Timeliness under stress as well as in normal conditions.** Aggregation systems must produce critical risk data rapidly in a stress or crisis, with some position and exposure data available intraday (¶45: *"Banks need to build their risk systems to be capable of producing aggregated risk data rapidly during times of stress/crisis for all critical risks."*)

- **Measurable, monitored, and escalated data quality.** Banks must not merely aspire to data quality; they must measure it, monitor it, and have escalation channels and action plans when quality degrades (¶40: *"Supervisors expect banks to measure and monitor the accuracy of data and to develop appropriate escalation channels and action plans to be in place to rectify poor data quality."*; ¶43: *"Supervisors expect banks to produce aggregated risk data that is complete and to measure and monitor the completeness of their risk data."*)

- **Documented data lineage and ownership with clear accountability.** Roles and responsibilities for data ownership and quality must be assigned to both business and IT functions, covering the full data lifecycle, and all aggregation processes — automated or manual — must be documented (¶34: *"Roles and responsibilities should be established as they relate to the ownership and quality of risk data and information for both the business and IT functions."*; ¶39: *"Supervisors expect banks to document and explain all of their risk data aggregation processes whether automated or manual."*)

### Who it applies to

BCBS 239 applies to **Global Systemically Important Banks (G-SIBs)** from its original compliance date (January 2016), with the Basel Committee expecting national supervisors to apply equivalent expectations to **Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIBs)** in due course. The principles address the consolidated banking group — including subsidiaries, legal entities, and off-balance-sheet vehicles — not merely a single legal entity or booking system.

## 2. Critical Data Element Candidates

---

**CDE-01 — Counterparty Identifier**
- **Definition:** The unique, group-wide identifier that unambiguously names a single legal-entity counterparty (borrower, issuer, derivative counterparty, deposit taker) across all systems and booking locations. This is the identifier used to link exposures from multiple business lines and legal entities to a single obligor.
- **Why critical:** Without a resolvable, consistent counterparty identifier, the bank cannot aggregate total exposure to a single name — the most fundamental credit aggregation act. It is also the primary joining key for concentration risk and large-exposure reporting. Fragmentation here directly distorts every credit, counterparty, and concentration risk figure.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 — Failure to resolve this element makes group-level risk aggregation structurally impossible, not merely inaccurate.
- **Driven by:** Principle 2 (¶33) — *"use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities, counterparties, customers and accounts"*; Principle 4 (¶41) — *"risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures"*; Principle 5 (¶46(b)) — *"Counterparty credit risk exposures, including, for example, derivatives"*
- **Search terms:** counterparty ID, obligor ID, party identifier, legal entity identifier, LEI, counterparty master, entity ID, client ID, BIC, borrower ID
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *uniqueness* — Each real-world counterparty maps to exactly one group-level identifier; no two records should carry different identifiers for the same legal entity | count of counterparties with more than one active group-level identifier in the master reference | target: zero duplicates
  - *completeness* — Every exposure record carries a populated, non-null counterparty identifier | count of exposure records with null or unresolvable counterparty identifier | target: 0% null; exceptions documented with materiality assessment
  - *consistency* — The counterparty identifier resolves to the same entity name and jurisdiction in every system where it appears | count of counterparty IDs that return conflicting entity names or jurisdictions across systems | target: zero inconsistencies in critical systems
  - *validity* — The identifier exists in and matches the authoritative counterparty master or LEI register | count of counterparty IDs on exposure records that are absent from the master reference | target: zero unmatched IDs in production

---

**CDE-02 — Legal Entity Identifier (Booking Entity)**
- **Definition:** The identifier of the bank's own legal entity in which a given exposure or position is booked. This is distinct from the counterparty's identifier; it identifies *which part of the banking group* holds the risk.
- **Why critical:** Without reliable booking-entity attribution, the bank cannot produce the legal-entity dimension of risk aggregation required by Principle 4, cannot assess intra-group concentration, and cannot comply with jurisdictional reporting requirements. It is also essential for business continuity planning under Principle 2.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, liquidity, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 — It is a structural joining key for every aggregation slice the regulation mandates.
- **Driven by:** Principle 2 (¶33) — *"single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities"*; Principle 4 (¶41–43) — data aggregation must be possible across the banking group including off-balance-sheet entities
- **Search terms:** legal entity ID, booking entity, entity code, subsidiary code, branch code, cost centre entity, reporting unit, LEI (own entity), legal vehicle ID
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *completeness* — Every exposure and position record is attributed to a booking legal entity | count of records with null or unrecognised booking entity | target: 0% null
  - *validity* — Every booking entity code resolves to an active entry in the group legal entity hierarchy | count of entity codes on risk records that are absent from or inactive in the group hierarchy | target: zero mismatches
  - *consistency* — The same legal entity is described by the same code in the risk system, the general ledger, and the regulatory reporting system | count of entity codes that differ across the three systems for the same economic entity | target: zero inconsistencies

---

**CDE-03 — Gross Exposure Amount**
- **Definition:** The pre-mitigation monetary value of a bank's exposure to a counterparty or instrument, expressed in transaction currency. This is the raw risk quantum before collateral, netting, or credit risk mitigation is applied.
- **Why critical:** It is the primary numeric input to credit aggregation, large-exposure calculation, and concentration analysis. Misstatement or incompleteness at this level directly distorts every downstream risk figure. Principle 3 requires reconciliation of risk data to accounting sources; gross exposure is the primary reconcilable quantity.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, concentration
- **Criticality:** 3 — It is both a calculation driver (Principle 7) and a reconciliation anchor (Principle 3).
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (¶36(c)) — *"Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate, to ensure that the risk data is accurate"*; Principle 4 (¶41) — *"risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures, including those that are off-balance sheet"*
- **Search terms:** drawn amount, outstanding balance, notional amount, exposure at default, EAD, committed amount, limit utilisation, principal balance, face value, nominal value
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *accuracy* — The sum of gross exposure amounts in the risk system reconciles to the corresponding balance in the general ledger or system of record within an approved tolerance | monetary difference between risk system aggregate and GL aggregate by portfolio segment | target: within materiality threshold defined by the bank's accuracy policy (¶56)
  - *completeness* — Every on- and off-balance-sheet facility has a populated gross exposure amount | count of facilities with null or zero exposure amount where a non-zero balance is expected | target: 0% unexplained nulls
  - *validity* — Exposure amounts are positive numbers in a recognised currency | count of records with negative, non-numeric, or currency-less exposure amounts | target: zero invalid values
  - *timeliness* — Exposure amounts reflect the position as of the required reporting date | age of the most recent exposure record per facility relative to the required as-of date | target: within the frequency window set for each risk type (¶44–47)

---

**CDE-04 — Risk Classification / Risk Type Code**
- **Definition:** The standardised code that assigns a given exposure or position to a risk category (e.g., credit, market, liquidity, operational) and, within credit, to a sub-type (e.g., corporate, retail, sovereign, financial institution). This is the classification that determines which aggregation bucket an exposure enters.
- **Why critical:** It drives all risk-type-level aggregation. An exposure miscoded as retail rather than corporate escapes large-exposure rules; one miscoded as credit rather than counterparty credit risk evades the wrong capital treatment. Principle 7 requires that reports "accurately and precisely convey aggregated risk data."
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, market, operational, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 — It is a direct calculation driver for capital, limits, and regulatory reports.
- **Driven by:** Principle 7 (¶52) — *"Risk management reports should be accurate and precise to ensure a bank's board and senior management can rely with confidence on the aggregated information"*; Principle 4 (¶42) — *"each system should make clear the specific approach used to aggregate exposures for any given risk measure"*
- **Search terms:** risk type code, asset class, exposure class, Basel asset class, product type, instrument type, portfolio classification, sub-portfolio code, risk category
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *validity* — Every exposure or position record carries a risk classification code that exists in the approved reference list | count of records with risk classification codes not present in the authoritative code list | target: zero invalid codes
  - *completeness* — No exposure or position record is unclassified | count of records with null or placeholder risk classification | target: 0% null, with materiality-based exception reporting
  - *consistency* — The same instrument is assigned the same risk classification in the risk system and in the regulatory reporting system | count of instruments with differing classification codes across the two systems | target: zero unexplained differences

---

**CDE-05 — Instrument / Facility Identifier**
- **Definition:** The unique identifier for an individual credit facility, loan, bond, derivative contract, or off-balance-sheet commitment. It is the atomic record to which exposure amounts, counterparties, and risk classifications are attached.
- **Why critical:** It is the joining key that links the exposure amount to its counterparty, booking entity, and risk classification. Without it, no aggregation is possible; with duplicates or gaps, exposures are double-counted or lost. Principle 2 requires single identifiers; Principle 4 requires completeness including off-balance-sheet items.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, market, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 3 — Structural joining key; its failure propagates errors across all CDEs.
- **Driven by:** Principle 2 (¶33) — *"use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including… accounts"*; Principle 3 (¶36(d)) — *"A bank should strive towards a single authoritative source for risk data per each type of risk"*
- **Search terms:** deal ID, facility ID, loan ID, contract ID, position ID, trade ID, account number, instrument ID, ISIN, CUSIP, internal reference number
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *uniqueness* — Each instrument or facility has exactly one active identifier in the authoritative system; no two records represent the same instrument with different identifiers | count of instruments with more than one active identifier in the master source | target: zero duplicates
  - *completeness* — Every exposure and position record carries a populated instrument identifier | count of risk records with null instrument identifier | target: 0% null
  - *validity* — Every instrument identifier on a risk record resolves to an active entry in the instrument master | count of identifiers on risk records absent from the instrument master | target: zero unresolved references

---

**CDE-06 — Collateral Value and Collateral Type**
- **Definition:** The current assessed value of collateral pledged against an exposure, together with the standardised code for the type of collateral (e.g., cash, financial collateral, real estate, guarantee). These two elements together determine net exposure after credit risk mitigation.
- **Why critical:** Gross exposure alone is insufficient for risk aggregation when collateral applies; net exposure drives capital requirements and limit calculations. Stale or misclassified collateral values directly distort net credit risk figures. Principle 3 demands reconciliation to sources; collateral values are a primary reconciliation target against collateral management systems.
- **Risk types:** Credit, counterparty, concentration
- **Criticality:** 2 — Important for net exposure accuracy but derived from a supporting collateral management system rather than being the primary exposure record itself.
- **Driven by:** Principle 3 (¶36(c)) — *"Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate"*; Principle 7 (¶53(a)) — *"Defined requirements and processes to reconcile reports to risk data"*
- **Search terms:** collateral value, market value of collateral, haircut, collateral type, eligible collateral, security value, LTV, loan-to-value, pledge amount, guarantee amount, credit support annex, CSA value
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *timeliness* — Collateral values are refreshed at a frequency consistent with the volatility of the collateral type (daily for financial collateral, periodic for real estate) | age of the most recent collateral valuation per collateral record relative to the required reporting date | target: within frequency window approved per collateral type
  - *accuracy* — Collateral values reconcile to the collateral management system or valuation source | monetary difference between risk system collateral values and collateral management system values | target: within approved reconciliation tolerance
  - *validity* — Collateral type codes exist in the approved reference list | count of records with collateral type codes absent from the approved list | target: zero invalid codes

---

**CDE-07 — Country / Jurisdiction of Risk**
- **Definition:** The standardised country code representing where the risk is domiciled — typically the country of the ultimate obligor or the country of the underlying asset, as relevant to credit and concentration risk. This is the "country risk" dimension, not the booking country.
- **Why critical:** Principle 4 explicitly requires aggregation by geography. Principle 6 (¶50) specifically names country credit exposure aggregation as an example of a mandatory ad hoc capability. Miscoded country risk makes geographic concentration invisible.
- **Risk types:** Credit, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 — Critical for concentration and stress reporting but downstream of the primary exposure record.
- **Driven by:** Principle 4 (¶41–43) — data must be available *"by… region and other groupings"*; Principle 6 (¶50) — *"a bank should be able to aggregate risk data quickly on country credit exposures as of a specified date based on a list of countries"*
- **Search terms:** country of risk, country code, ISO country code, obligor country, domicile, country of incorporation, sovereign exposure country, transfer risk country, geographic region
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *validity* — Every country code is a recognised ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 or alpha-3 code, or maps to the bank's approved geographic hierarchy | count of country codes on risk records that are absent from the approved country reference list | target: zero invalid codes
  - *completeness* — Every exposure record carries a populated country-of-risk code | count of exposure records with null or generic country code | target: 0% null, with exceptions documented
  - *consistency* — The country of risk is assigned on the basis of a documented and consistently applied methodology (e.g., country of ultimate obligor, not booking country) | count of records where the assignment basis differs from the documented standard | target: zero policy breaches

---

**CDE-08 — Industry / Sector Classification**
- **Definition:** The standardised code identifying the industry or economic sector of the obligor or underlying asset (e.g., NACE, GICS, SIC, or internal sector taxonomy). Used to aggregate sector concentration risk.
- **Why critical:** Principle 4 requires aggregation by industry; Principle 6 (¶50) specifically names industry credit exposure aggregation as a mandatory ad hoc capability. Sector concentration risk — particularly in stress scenarios — depends entirely on the accuracy and completeness of this field.
- **Risk types:** Credit, concentration
- **Criticality:** 2 — Essential for concentration aggregation but less structurally foundational than counterparty or entity identifiers.
- **Driven by:** Principle 4 (¶41) — data available *"by… industry"*; Principle 6 (¶50) — *"industry credit exposures as of a specified date based on a list of industry types across all business lines and geographic areas"*
- **Search terms:** industry code, sector code, NACE code, SIC code, GICS sector, obligor industry, borrower sector, sector classification, economic sector
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *validity* — Industry codes conform to the bank's approved sector taxonomy or an accepted external standard | count of records with industry codes absent from the approved reference list | target: zero invalid codes
  - *completeness* — Every material exposure carries a populated industry code | count of material exposures with null industry code | target: below materiality threshold with exceptions documented
  - *consistency* — The same obligor is assigned the same industry code across credit risk and market risk systems | count of obligors with differing industry codes across systems | target: zero unexplained differences

---

**CDE-09 — Business Line / Desk Code**
- **Definition:** The standardised identifier that assigns a risk exposure or position to an organisational unit within the bank — typically a business line, division, or trading desk. It is the internal segmentation dimension for risk aggregation.
- **Why critical:** Principle 4 requires aggregation by business line. Without a reliable, consistently applied business line code, the bank cannot produce business-line-level risk views, cannot enforce business-line limits, and cannot detect concentrations forming within a single unit.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, operational, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 — Required for the business-line dimension of aggregation but generally more stable and less prone to integrity failures than counterparty or exposure identifiers.
- **Driven by:** Principle 4 (¶41) — data must be available *"by business line"*; Principle 2 (¶34) — roles and responsibilities for data quality attach to business owners
- **Search terms:** business line code, division code, desk code, cost centre, profit centre, segment code, organisational unit, business unit, product line
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *validity* — Business line codes exist in the approved organisational hierarchy | count of records with codes absent from the current hierarchy | target: zero invalid codes
  - *completeness* — Every risk record carries a populated business line code | count of records with null or default business line code | target: 0% null
  - *timeliness* — Business line codes are updated promptly following organisational restructuring | lag between effective date of restructuring and update of codes on affected records | target: within the change management window defined in the bank's data governance policy

---

**CDE-10 — Risk Limit and Limit Utilisation**
- **Definition:** The approved risk limit for a given counterparty, portfolio, country, or business line, expressed in the same metric as the exposure (e.g., monetary amount, VaR units, percentage of capital), paired with the current utilisation of that limit. Together they express the bank's risk appetite operationally.
- **Why critical:** Principle 8 (¶58) requires risk reports to *"provide information in the context of limits and risk appetite/tolerance."* Limit and utilisation data are the primary mechanism by which the board and senior management monitor adherence to risk appetite. Stale, incomplete, or inconsistent limit data makes risk appetite monitoring meaningless.
- **Risk types:** Credit, market, liquidity, counterparty, concentration, cross-cutting
- **Criticality:** 2 — Critical for risk appetite reporting but dependent on the accuracy of exposure CDEs already listed.
- **Driven by:** Principle 7 (¶52–53) — reports must be accurate enough to support critical decisions; Principle 8 (¶58) — *"Reports should identify emerging risk concentrations, provide information in the context of limits and risk appetite/tolerance"*
- **Search terms:** limit amount, approved limit, credit limit, trading limit, VaR limit, utilisation, headroom, limit usage, exposure vs limit, limit breach, risk appetite metric
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *accuracy* — Limit utilisation amounts are calculated from the same exposure data that feeds the primary risk report, with no shadow or competing calculation | count of limit utilisation figures that differ from the primary risk system exposure for the same dimension | target: zero unexplained differences
  - *timeliness* — Limits and utilisation figures are current as of the reporting date; limits reflect the most recently approved version | age of limit record relative to the most recent approval record | target: within the reporting frequency window
  - *completeness* — Every material risk dimension covered by the bank's risk appetite framework has a populated limit record | count of risk appetite dimensions with no corresponding limit record | target: 0% gaps at material threshold

---

**CDE-11 — Liquidity Risk Indicator (Cash Flow / Funding Position)**
- **Definition:** The scheduled or projected cash inflow or outflow, and the corresponding funding position or buffer, for a defined time bucket (e.g., overnight, 1-week, 30-day). These are the primary inputs to liquidity risk aggregation and stress testing.
- **Why critical:** Principle 5 (¶46(d)) explicitly names *"Liquidity risk indicators such as cash flows/settlements and funding"* as critical risks requiring rapid aggregation in stress. The 2008 crisis demonstrated that banks that could not rapidly aggregate their intraday and short-term liquidity positions could not respond to a funding shock.
- **Risk types:** Liquidity
- **Criticality:** 3 — Explicitly named by the regulation as requiring rapid aggregation under stress; failure directly impairs crisis response.
- **Driven by:** Principle 5 (¶46(d)) — *"Liquidity risk indicators such as cash flows/settlements and funding"*; Principle 5 (¶45) — *"Banks need to build their risk systems to be capable of producing aggregated risk data rapidly during times of stress/crisis for all critical risks"*
- **Search terms:** cash flow, net cash flow, settlement amount, funding requirement, liquidity buffer, LCR, NSFR, HQLA, contractual cash flow, liquidity time bucket, intraday liquidity, collateral available
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *timeliness* — Cash flow and funding position data are available at intraday frequency for stress reporting; at minimum daily for normal reporting | lag between position date and availability of aggregated liquidity figures | target: intraday for stress-designated positions; end-of-day T+0 for normal reporting
  - *completeness* — All material funding sources and cash flow obligations are captured, including committed but undrawn facilities and contingent outflows | count of known funding lines or contractual obligations absent from the liquidity aggregation | target: 0% gap at material threshold
  - *accuracy* — Aggregate net cash flow position reconciles to treasury or ALM system | monetary difference between risk aggregation and treasury system for each time bucket | target: within approved tolerance

---

**CDE-12 — Market Risk Position / Mark-to-Market Value**
- **Definition:** The current fair value of a trading position or portfolio, together with its sensitivity measures (e.g., DV01, delta, VaR contribution). This is the primary input to market risk aggregation, limit monitoring, and P&L attribution.
- **Why critical:** Principle 5 (¶46(c)) explicitly names *"Trading exposures, positions, operating limits, and market concentrations by sector and region data"* as critical risks. Market risk positions can change intraday; stale valuations directly distort VaR, stress test results, and limit utilisation.
- **Risk types:** Market, counterparty
- **Criticality:** 3 — Explicitly named as a critical risk; positions can move materially intraday, making timeliness as important as accuracy.
- **Driven by:** Principle 5 (¶46(c)) — *"Trading exposures, positions, operating limits, and market concentrations by sector and region data"*; Principle 3 (¶36(c)) — reconciliation to authoritative sources required
- **Search terms:** mark-to-market value, MTM, fair value, position value, PV01, DV01, delta, VaR, stressed VaR, net position, trading book position, market value
- **Data quality requirements:**
  - *timeliness* — Mark-to-market values are refreshed at the frequency required by the bank's market risk reporting cycle, with intraday capability for stress | age of the most recent valuation per position relative to the required reporting frequency | target: end-of-day T+0 for normal; intraday on demand for stress
  - *accuracy* — Position valuations reconcile to the front-office system of record | monetary difference between risk aggregation values and front-office system values | target: within approved price tolerance per instrument type
  - *completeness* — Every open trading position is included in the aggregated market risk figures | count of front-office positions absent from the risk aggregation | target: zero missing positions above materiality threshold

## 3. Cross-Cutting Data Quality Requirements

These requirements span multiple CDEs and cannot be attached to any single element. They emerge from the regulation's demand for reconciliation, consistent taxonomy, and group-wide completeness.

---

### XDQ-01 — Risk-to-Finance Reconciliation

**Requirement intent:** Risk exposure aggregates must reconcile to the general ledger and financial accounting records. Where differences exist, they must be explained and documented. This is not optional; it is the mechanism by which Principle 3's accuracy requirement is demonstrated and audited.

**Measurement:** Monetary difference between the sum of gross exposure amounts in the risk aggregation system and the corresponding balances in the general ledger, computed at agreed segmentation levels (by legal entity, by product type, by booking date).

**Suggested threshold:** Within the materiality threshold defined in the bank's accuracy policy (¶56); all differences above threshold are documented with root-cause and remediation timeline.

**CDEs spanned:** CDE-03 (Gross Exposure Amount), CDE-02 (Legal Entity), CDE-05 (Instrument / Facility Identifier), CDE-01 (Counterparty Identifier)

**Driven by:** Principle 3 (¶36(c)) — *"Risk data should be reconciled with bank's sources, including accounting data where appropriate"*; Principle 7 (¶53(a)) — *"Defined requirements and processes to reconcile reports to risk data"*

---

### XDQ-02 — Cross-System Identifier Consistency

**Requirement intent:** The same counterparty, legal entity, or instrument must carry the same identifier across all systems that contribute to risk aggregation — front office, risk, finance, and regulatory reporting. Identifier fragmentation is the single most common cause of aggregation failure. This is the operational expression of ¶33's "single identifiers" requirement.

**Measurement:** Count of counterparties, entities, or instruments that carry different identifiers for the same economic object across any two contributing systems; count of cross-system joins that fail due to unmatched identifiers.

**Suggested threshold:** Zero unresolved mismatches for counterparties and instruments above the materiality threshold; documented reconciliation for all others.

**CDEs spanned:** CDE-01 (Counterparty Identifier), CDE-02 (Legal Entity Identifier), CDE-05 (Instrument / Facility Identifier)

**Driven by:** Principle 2 (¶33) — *"use of single identifiers and/or unified naming conventions for data including legal entities, counterparties, customers and accounts"*

---

### XDQ-03 — Completeness of Off-Balance-Sheet Capture

**Requirement intent:** Risk aggregation must include off-balance-sheet exposures — undrawn commitments, guarantees, letters of credit, derivatives — as well as on-balance-sheet positions. The regulation explicitly calls this out because off-balance-sheet items are the most common source of hidden concentration.

**Measurement:** Count and value of known off-balance-sheet facilities (drawn from the commitment management or derivatives system) that are absent from the risk aggregation; ratio of off-balance-sheet facilities represented in risk aggregation to total known off-balance-sheet population.

**Suggested threshold:** 100% capture of off-balance-sheet facilities above individual materiality threshold; aggregate coverage ratio monitored monthly.

**CDEs spanned:** CDE-03 (Gross Exposure Amount), CDE-05 (Instrument / Facility Identifier), CDE-01 (Counterparty Identifier), CDE-04 (Risk Classification)

**Driven by:** Principle 4 (¶41) — *"A bank's risk data aggregation capabilities should include all material risk exposures, including those that are off-balance sheet"*

---

### XDQ-04 — Reference Data Consistency Across the Group Taxonomy

**Requirement intent:** Country codes, industry codes, business line codes, and risk classification codes must mean the same thing in every system and every legal entity across the group. A country code that means "country of booking" in one system and "country of ultimate obligor" in another makes geographic aggregation meaningless. This is the data dictionary requirement of ¶37 operationalised.

**Measurement:** Count of reference code values that have different definitions or mappings in different systems; count of systems that use a non-standard variant of a group-approved code list.

**Suggested threshold:** Zero definitional inconsistencies for codes used in mandatory regulatory aggregations; all deviations documented with a mapping and remediation plan.

**CDEs spanned:** CDE-04 (Risk Classification), CDE-07 (Country / Jurisdiction), CDE-08 (Industry / Sector), CDE-09 (Business Line / Desk Code)

**Driven by:** Principle 2 (¶33) — *"integrated data taxonomies and architecture across the banking group"*; Principle 3 (¶37) — *"a bank should have a 'dictionary' of the concepts used, such that data is defined consistently across an organisation"*

---

### XDQ-05 — Stress-Scenario Data Readiness

**Requirement intent:** The bank must be able to produce complete, accurate, and timely aggregated risk data under stress conditions — not merely under normal conditions. This requires that the CDEs supporting stress scenarios (particularly those for credit, liquidity, and market risk) are available at the required speed and with documented fallback procedures where automated processes fail.

**Measurement:** Time elapsed between a declared stress event and the production of a complete aggregated risk report covering credit, market, and liquidity positions; count of CDEs for which the stress-scenario refresh lag exceeds the approved stress SLA.

**Suggested threshold:** All critical CDEs available within the stress SLA defined by senior management; zero CDEs above the materiality threshold with an undefined stress SLA.

**CDEs spanned:** CDE-03 (Gross Exposure), CDE-10 (Limit Utilisation), CDE-11 (Liquidity Cash Flow), CDE-12 (Market Risk Position)

**Driven by:** Principle 2 (¶32) — *"Risk data aggregation capabilities and risk reporting practices should be given direct consideration as part of a bank's business continuity planning processes"*; Principle 5 (¶45) — *"Banks need to build their risk systems to be capable of producing aggregated risk data rapidly during times of stress/crisis"*

## 4. Out of Scope — What a Data Catalog Cannot Deliver

This section identifies the principles and requirements that no CDE register, metadata catalog, or data quality monitoring programme can satisfy on its own. Stating this plainly is necessary because a catalog that claims to solve the whole regulation is a catalog that misleads its owners.

---

### Principles 8–11: Reporting Comprehensiveness, Clarity, Frequency, and Distribution

**What the regulation demands (¶57–73):** Risk management reports must cover all material risk areas; present data in context of limits and risk appetite; be clear and useful to their specific recipients; be produced at appropriate frequency including intraday in stress; and be distributed to the right people while maintaining confidentiality.

**Why a catalog cannot deliver this:** These are requirements about the *content, format, recipients, and cadence of management information* — not about the quality or governance of the underlying data elements. A data catalog can document what data exists, who owns it, and whether it meets quality standards. It cannot:
- Determine whether a risk report is *comprehensible* or *useful* to a board member (Principle 9)
- Assess whether the *right risk areas* are covered in a given report or whether emerging risks are being surfaced (Principle 8, ¶58–60)
- Enforce or monitor *report distribution* or access controls on finished reports (Principle 11)
- Set or enforce *reporting frequency* — that is a governance and operational decision requiring report scheduling, workflow, and escalation infrastructure (Principle 10)
- Verify that forward-looking forecasts and stress-test narratives are appropriate (¶60, ¶62)

**What is needed instead:** A risk reporting governance framework covering report inventories, recipient matrices, frequency schedules, and board/senior management sign-off processes. This is typically owned by the Risk Reporting function in partnership with the Chief Risk Officer and Board Risk Committee. A data catalog can *support* it by providing the data lineage and quality evidence that reports can cite, but the catalog is an input, not a substitute.

---

### Principle 1: Board and Senior Management Governance Accountability

**What the regulation demands (¶27–31):** The board must review and approve the risk data aggregation framework; senior management must understand the limitations of aggregation; the framework must be independently validated; and acquisitions must trigger a reassessment of data capabilities.

**Why a catalog cannot deliver this:** These are board-level governance and accountability requirements. A catalog can:
- Provide the *documented evidence* that independent validation can inspect (¶29(a))
- Surface the known limitations of data coverage that senior management must understand (¶30)
- Record data ownership assignments (¶34)

But it cannot:
- Constitute or substitute for board approval of the framework
- Perform or mandate independent validation
- Trigger or govern the due diligence process for an acquisition (¶29(b))
- Ensure senior management is "fully aware" of aggregation limitations — that requires active governance engagement, not a catalog entry

**What is needed instead:** A formal data governance framework with documented board approval, an independent model and data validation function, and change management processes that include data capability assessment as a gating criterion for acquisitions.

---

### Principle 3: Manual Process Controls and End-User Computing

**What the regulation demands (¶36(b), ¶38–39):** Where risk aggregation relies on manual processes, spreadsheets, or end-user computing tools, effective mitigants and controls must be in place, documented, and consistently applied.

**Why a catalog cannot deliver this:** A catalog can document that manual processes exist and flag them as a data quality risk. It cannot:
- Control or audit the operation of a spreadsheet
- Enforce end-user computing policies
- Test or validate the accuracy of a manual workaround
- Reduce reliance on manual processes — that requires systems investment

**What is needed instead:** An end-user computing (EUC) control framework, typically owned by Internal Audit or Operational Risk, covering inventory of critical spreadsheets, version control, access restriction, and periodic testing.

---

### Principle 7: Reasonableness Checks, Validation Rules, and Exception Reporting

**What the regulation demands (¶53(b)–(c)):** Banks must maintain an inventory of validation rules applied to quantitative information in reports, including mathematical and logical relationship checks, and must have integrated procedures for identifying, reporting, and explaining data errors through exceptions reports.

**Why a catalog cannot — fully — deliver this:** A data catalog can host a *reference inventory* of validation rules and link them to CDEs. However:
- The *execution* of those validation rules happens in risk systems, reporting engines, or data pipelines — not in the catalog
- The *exceptions reporting* process (escalation, root-cause, remediation sign-off) is an operational workflow that requires case management capability beyond typical catalog functionality
- The catalog cannot enforce that validation rules are actually applied in production systems

**What is needed instead:** A data quality rules engine or integrated monitoring layer (which may be connected to the catalog) that executes checks, generates exceptions, routes them to data owners, and tracks remediation. The catalog's role is to be the authoritative register of *what rules exist and who owns them*, not to run them.
